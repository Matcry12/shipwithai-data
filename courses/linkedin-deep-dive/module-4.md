# Module 4 — Featured, Recommendations & Profile Completeness

**Objective:** Turn the discoverability signals you don't see into ones that work for you.

Most LinkedIn optimization advice focuses on the headline and the About section. Those matter, but they are also the sections every tutorial covers. This module is about the three levers that the majority of working professionals leave untouched: the Featured section, a custom profile URL, and recommendations. Each one takes under an hour to fix and each one directly affects how often LinkedIn surfaces your profile to recruiters and hiring managers.

---

## Lesson 1 — The Featured Section: Free Real Estate You're Probably Ignoring

Scroll down past the About section on almost any LinkedIn profile and you will find one of two things: a populated Featured section showing polished work samples, or a blank gap where that section could be. The blank gap is the default. Most people never touch it.

That is a measurable mistake. Profiles with a completed Featured section can receive up to 30% more views, because the content keeps visitors on your page longer and signals authority to LinkedIn's algorithm, which in 2026 explicitly prioritizes dwell time and engagement as ranking signals[^1].

The Featured section sits directly below your About section and accepts LinkedIn posts, external links, uploaded media files, and articles. For a tech professional optimizing for inbound opportunities, the highest-value items to pin are:

- **Your resume as a PDF.** A recruiter who lands on your profile can download it in one click[^2]. No DM required, no friction.
- **A portfolio link or live project.** A GitHub repository, a deployed app, a Notion case study — anything that proves the work[^3].
- **One or two specific work samples.** A slide deck, a one-page project overview, or a video walkthrough. Use the Media upload option to attach PDFs or decks directly[^1].

Keep the list to 2–5 items. More than that overwhelms the visitor. Order matters — LinkedIn shows the first item most prominently, so put your strongest piece first[^3]. Update the section quarterly; stale content signals a stale career.

The Featured section is described in one guide as "prime visual space that most profiles waste"[^4]. Once a recruiter has clicked through to your profile, it does a better job of proving your expertise than text alone, because it lets them see the work rather than read claims about it[^5].

**Action:** Add your Featured section today. Pin your resume PDF, one external portfolio or project link, and one work sample. Use "Add profile section" > "Recommended" > "Add featured."

## Lesson 2 — Custom URL: A 60-Second Fix With Lasting Returns

When LinkedIn creates your profile, it assigns a URL that looks something like `linkedin.com/in/yourname-9315a3202`. That string of numbers at the end is not accidental ugliness — it is LinkedIn's default identifier. Almost no one changes it.

Changing it to `linkedin.com/in/yourname` takes about sixty seconds and produces three concrete benefits:

1. **It is shareable.** You can put it in your email signature, on your resume header, or in a job application without it looking like a system-generated string[^6].
2. **It is searchable.** A custom URL helps search engines like Google index your profile more effectively, making you discoverable beyond LinkedIn's own search[^1].
3. **It signals professionalism.** The default URL with its trailing numbers reads as an unconfigured profile. A clean URL — especially one consistent across GitHub, Twitter, and other platforms — reads as intentional[^7].

Steps to change it: click "Me" at the top of LinkedIn, select "View Profile," then click "Edit public profile & URL" in the top right. Under "Edit your custom URL," click the pencil icon, type your preferred alias, and save[^8]. If your name is taken, add a middle initial or a professional abbreviation.

Use your name, or a version of your professional name that you also use elsewhere. A custom LinkedIn URL that includes your name (e.g., `linkedin.com/in/johndoe`) is specifically recommended as standard contact information to include on a resume[^9].

**Action:** Go to your LinkedIn profile right now and customize your URL. Aim for `linkedin.com/in/firstname-lastname`. Then update your resume header and email signature to match.

## Lesson 3 — Recommendations: Quality Over Quantity

Endorsements — the one-click skill tags — are largely noise. Recruiters know they require no effort. Recommendations are different. A written recommendation attached to a real person with a real title is social proof that carries weight.

The goal is not volume. Two or three strong recommendations from direct managers or close collaborators outperform ten vague ones from distant connections. The reason is specificity: a recommendation that names a project, describes a concrete outcome, and comes from someone whose title confirms relevance ("Engineering Manager at X") tells a recruiter something an endorsement never can.

The main reason most profiles have weak or no recommendations is friction — people don't know what to ask for, and the person they ask doesn't know what to write. You can eliminate both problems:

**Who to ask:** Your most recent direct manager is the highest-value source. A senior colleague who worked alongside you on a visible project is second. Aim for people whose titles are recognizable in your target industry.

**How to ask:** Send a short, direct message. Reference a specific project or time period so the writer has a concrete starting point. Offer to draft a starter paragraph they can edit — most people appreciate this and it dramatically increases follow-through.

**Starter draft you can send:**

> "Hi [Name], I'm updating my LinkedIn profile and would really value a recommendation from you, especially around our work on [Project/Team/Initiative]. I know you're busy, so I drafted a starting point you're welcome to edit or rewrite entirely:
>
> *'[Your Name] brought [specific skill] to [project context]. One thing that stood out was [concrete example]. I'd recommend them highly for roles that require [relevant capability].'*
>
> No pressure either way — just wanted to make it as easy as possible."

This approach — asking with context and providing a draft — converts at a much higher rate than a generic request. It respects the writer's time and gives them something to react to rather than a blank page.

Make sure to include endorsements and recommendations to build the trust needed to turn a profile view into a real opportunity[^1]. Credibility signals like recommendations are specifically identified as a way to increase your profile's impact with recruiters[^8].

**Action:** Identify two people who could write you a strong recommendation. Send one request this week using the draft template above. Then request the second within the following week.

---

## Module Summary

The Featured section, your custom URL, and a handful of quality recommendations are the three highest-leverage profile elements that most working professionals leave empty or at their default state. A completed Featured section increases dwell time and profile views; a clean custom URL makes you shareable and searchable; and two or three specific recommendations from credible sources provide the social proof that a keyword-optimized profile alone cannot. None of these changes require new work — they surface and organize proof that already exists. Combined with the keyword and headline work from earlier modules, they move your profile from technically complete to genuinely competitive for inbound opportunities.
