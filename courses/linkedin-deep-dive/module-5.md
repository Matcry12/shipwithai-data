# Module 5 — Open to Work, Visibility, and the Recruiter Inbound Loop

**Objective:** Configure the settings that put you in front of recruiters who are actively searching.

Most LinkedIn optimization advice stops at profile content — headline, About, Featured. But content is only half the equation. The other half is configuration: the privacy and preference settings that control whether recruiters can find you at all, and on what terms. This module covers the four settings that directly feed the inbound loop: the Open to Work signal, your job preferences, your public search visibility, and the feedback mechanism that tells you whether any of it is working.

---

## Lesson 1 — Turning On Open to Work (and Choosing the Right Mode)

The Open to Work feature is a direct signal to recruiters that you are available. It is one of the most underused tools on LinkedIn among employed professionals, usually because people assume it will alert their current employer. That concern is valid — and LinkedIn has a setting for it.

There are two modes:

**All Members** adds the green "#OpenToWork" photo frame to your profile picture, visible to everyone on LinkedIn. This is the maximum-reach option — LinkedIn's algorithm actively tries to surface your profile to recruiters, starting with your first-degree connections. The trade-off: everyone can see you are looking, including your current employer and colleagues, and the public signal exposes you to MLM pitches, lowball contract offers, and LinkedIn scams.[^1]

**Recruiters Only** restricts the signal to companies using LinkedIn Recruiter, LinkedIn's enterprise sourcing tool. Ordinary members, including colleagues at your current company, cannot see it.[^1] This mode is designed for employed professionals who want inbound interest without broadcasting their search — if you're employed, this is the recommended default to avoid awkward conversations with your current employer.[^2] LinkedIn explicitly notes that even with this setting on, it cannot guarantee complete privacy, so proceed with awareness rather than assumed safety.[^3]

To enable either mode, navigate to "Edit job preferences," fill in the required fields, then choose whether to share your status with all LinkedIn members or only with recruiters.[^3] One LinkedIn-specific framing worth noting: the green public badge is more visible and effective for attention, but it can hurt salary negotiations by signaling urgency. Use the public badge if you are unemployed or openly searching, and the private setting if you are employed and exploring discreetly.[^4]

The payoff is concrete. Turning on the Recruiters Only setting can increase your chance of appearing in recruiter searches by up to 40%.[^5] Even the original "Pro tip" from developers who have iterated on this points to the same default: turn it on, set it to recruiters only — the recruiter-only setting feeds you directly into their search filters without the negotiation downside.[^6]

**Action:** Enable Open to Work today. If you are employed, start with Recruiters Only. If you are actively searching and not concerned about visibility, switch to All Members. You can change the mode at any time without losing your preference data.

## Lesson 2 — Job Preferences: The Filter Recruiters Actually Use

Turning on Open to Work without filling in your preferences is like putting up a "for sale" sign with no price. The signal is there, but the information recruiters need to act on it is not.

LinkedIn's job preferences sit inside the same Open to Work setup flow. They matter because LinkedIn Recruiter is a premium tool that filters candidates by keywords, job title, location, industry, current and past companies, skills, years of experience, and education — your preferences feed directly into those filters.[^4] A recruiter searching for a "Senior Product Manager" open to "Remote" in "United States" will see you only if your preferences match those parameters.

The standard LinkedIn flow asks for job titles, location types, start date, and employment types — fill in every one.[^3]

**Job titles.** Enter the specific titles you are targeting, not a generic summary of your skills. "Senior Software Engineer," "Staff Engineer," and "Engineering Manager" are distinct searches. Add up to five titles and use the exact phrasing recruiters are likely to search — look at job postings for the roles you want and mirror their title language.

**Locations.** Add every geography you would genuinely consider. If you are open to multiple metros or fully remote from anywhere, list them. LinkedIn treats location as a hard filter in many recruiter searches, so a candidate who left this blank is invisible to location-specific queries even if everything else matches. One practical refinement: select the broadest range that still encompasses where you live or want to work — a metro area like "New York City Metropolitan Area" substantially expands the searches you appear in compared to a single city.[^3]

**Work arrangements.** Select from Remote, Hybrid, and On-Site — and select all that apply honestly. Recruiters frequently filter by arrangement type before looking at anything else. A candidate who is remote-only but left this blank will be surfaced in on-site searches and generate recruiter frustration when the mismatch surfaces later.

**Job types.** Full-time, part-time, contract, internship, temporary. Select what you are actually open to. If you are open to contract work in addition to full-time, say so — contract roles are often filled faster and can lead to permanent positions.

Think of job preferences as a standing query you set once on behalf of every recruiter who will ever search for someone like you. The more precisely you fill them in, the higher your match rate with relevant searches.

**Action:** Open your Open to Work preferences and complete every field — at minimum job titles, locations, and work arrangements. Revisit them quarterly or whenever your target role evolves.

## Lesson 3 — Public Visibility and the Profile View Feedback Loop

Two more settings sit outside the Open to Work flow but have a direct impact on how often you appear in front of relevant people.

**Profile public to search engines.** Set your LinkedIn profile to "Public" to maximize your visibility to recruiters and potential employers. Adjust your privacy settings so your profile, headline, and summary are visible to everyone — this lets recruiters evaluate your qualifications even when you are not actively searching. Customize the public profile to include your name and relevant keywords, making your URL easier to find and share.[^7] The setting lives under Settings & Privacy > Visibility > Edit your public profile.

Make sure your public profile is set to visible and that you have selected which sections appear publicly. At minimum, show your headline, current position, and a summary. The goal is enough information to confirm you are a credible candidate and prompt a click-through to your full profile.

**Search Appearances and Who Viewed Your Profile.** LinkedIn gives you a built-in analytics dashboard — most users never open it. Track Search Appearances weekly: it tells you how many times your profile appeared in LinkedIn searches. If it's trending down, your keywords need refreshing. If it spikes after profile edits, you've hit the right keywords.[^2]

Profile Views complements that signal. LinkedIn shows you viewer demographics including job titles, companies, and locations. If the wrong people are finding you, your keywords are attracting the wrong audience.[^2] After updating your About or headline, monitor profile views (frequency and visitor demographics), connection request rates, and InMail/message response rates — the deltas are your A/B test signal in a platform that doesn't offer true A/B testing.[^8]

If you see multiple recruiters from a specific company viewing your profile after you updated your headline to include a certain keyword, that keyword is working. If you changed your About section last month and profile views dropped, something in the new version is less searchable than the old one. If you are getting views from recruiters at companies three levels below your target seniority, your headline may be attracting the wrong searches.

Check who viewed your profile once a week while you are actively optimizing. Note the job titles of viewers, their companies, and whether there are clusters. Use that data to refine your headline and keyword choices from earlier modules. The profile view list is not a vanity metric — it is a direct readout of which parts of your profile are working as discovery surfaces and which are not.

**Action:** Go to Settings & Privacy > Visibility > Edit your public profile and confirm your profile is set to visible. Then check your "Who viewed your profile" list and note the job titles and companies represented. Identify one keyword or section that may need tuning based on what you see.

## Module Summary

The inbound recruiter loop depends on four interconnected settings: Open to Work (mode chosen to match your disclosure comfort), job preferences (titles, locations, and arrangements filled in as recruiter-ready filters), public search visibility (so Google surfaces your profile beyond LinkedIn's walls), and the profile view feedback loop (which tells you whether your keyword and headline choices are attracting the right searchers). None of these settings are set-and-forget. The professionals who generate consistent inbound interest treat them as a system — turning signals on, watching what comes back, and adjusting. Combined with the profile content work from earlier modules, these configurations move you from passively present on LinkedIn to actively visible to the people doing the hiring.
