# Module 6 — LinkedIn That Matches Your Resume

**Objective:** Make recruiters who search find you, and make the profile they land on confirm your resume.

---

## Lesson 6.1 — Your Headline Is a Search Query

Recruiters do not browse LinkedIn profiles at random. They run keyword searches and filter results. Your headline is the single highest-weight field in LinkedIn's search algorithm — it determines whether you appear at all.

LinkedIn gives you up to 220 characters in the headline field, but on mobile only the first 60–80 characters are visible. That means your primary role keyword must come first. Burying it at the end wastes the most valuable real estate on your profile.[^1]

A headline that works follows this structure:

**Role | Key Skills or Specialty | Outcome or Credibility**

Examples for junior tech roles:
- `Full-Stack Developer (JavaScript, Python) | AWS Cloud and DevOps | Open to SRE Roles`
- `Data Analyst | SQL, Python, Tableau | Seeking Entry-Level BI Roles`

Two things to notice: the role is spelled out in searchable terms, and the skills listed match what recruiters type into the search bar. Generic filler like "hard-working" or "dynamic leader" wastes characters — no one searches for those phrases. Every word should either be a keyword or a statement of value.[^1]

Your headline must also mirror the job title on your resume. If your resume says "Software Engineer," your headline should not say "Tech Wizard" or "Code Craftsman." Consistency is what lets recruiters verify they found the right person.

**Action:** Rewrite your headline using `Role | Specialty | Stack or Target Role` — front-load the job title and strip any adjective that isn't searchable.

## Lesson 6.2 — The About Section: Keywords Wrapped in a Narrative

Most job seekers either skip the About section or paste in a wall of text. Both are wrong. The About section is the largest keyword container on your profile — 2,600 characters — and it is the first substantial content block a recruiter reads after clicking your name.[^2]

The goal is to weave 8–12 relevant keywords into a short narrative, not to stuff them into a list. LinkedIn's algorithm scans for keyword density in this section, and a human has to find it worth reading once they arrive.[^1]

Structure your About section in four blocks:

1. **Hook** — One or two sentences on what you do and the kind of role you want. Be explicit. If you're a career-changer, say what you're moving toward, not just where you've been.[^1]
2. **Core skills and tools** — One short paragraph naming your primary technologies, methodologies, and industry terms. A web developer should mention specific frameworks; a data analyst should name the tools. These are the terms recruiters search for.
3. **Quantified achievements** — Two or three bullet points with numbers. Pull these directly from your resume. Repetition between your resume and LinkedIn is a feature, not a flaw — it signals consistency.
4. **Call to action** — One sentence telling the reader what to do next. "Open to full-time backend roles — feel free to reach out" is enough.

Write in first person. "I built" reads as more credible and human than "Responsible for building." Keep paragraphs to two or three sentences. On mobile, only the first three lines appear before the "See more" click — make those lines count.[^3]

Your About section should echo your resume summary word for word on the key terms, even if the phrasing differs. When a recruiter reads your resume and then opens your LinkedIn, they should feel like both documents are describing the same person.

**Action:** Draft your About section hook — two sentences that name your target role, your primary skill, and one quantified achievement from your resume.

## Lesson 6.3 — Experience, Profile Hygiene, and the Open to Work Toggle

**Experience section**

Each role entry in your Experience section gets 2,000 characters. Use them the same way you use your resume bullets: action verb, what you did, measurable result. Start each bullet with a strong verb and include three to five role-specific keywords per position.[^3]

The job title field carries significant search weight. If your actual title was something internal and non-standard — "Customer Happiness Engineer," "Growth Hacker," "Pod Lead" — add the standard industry equivalent in parentheses next to it. For example: `Customer Happiness Engineer (Customer Success Manager)`. Recruiters search for standard titles, not internal branding.[^1]

Your LinkedIn experience bullets do not need to be identical to your resume bullets, but they must be consistent. The same roles, the same dates, the same employers. Discrepancies between a resume and LinkedIn are a red flag that hiring managers notice.

**Profile hygiene: the non-negotiables**

Several profile elements affect how often your profile surfaces in searches and how credible it looks when recruiters land on it. These are not optional polish — they are baseline requirements:

- **Professional photo.** Profiles with photos receive significantly more views than those without.[^3]
- **Custom URL.** Change the default random-character URL to `linkedin.com/in/yourname`. It makes the link shareable on your resume and looks deliberate.
- **Featured section.** Pin your resume PDF here. It is prime space that most profiles leave empty.[^3]
- **Complete every section.** A complete profile is more likely to be surfaced by LinkedIn's recommendation algorithm.[^4]

**Open to Work**

Turn the "Open to Work" feature on. It signals to recruiters that you are actively available, which puts your profile into a filterable category. Enabling it increases your chance of appearing in recruiter searches by up to 40%.[^5]

You have two visibility options. "All LinkedIn members" adds a green `#OpenToWork` frame to your photo — maximum reach, but your current employer can see it. "Recruiters only" limits visibility to companies using LinkedIn Recruiter, the enterprise hiring tool — more discreet, and it targets the searches that matter most if you're currently employed.

When you enable it, fill out the job titles, preferred locations, and work arrangements (remote, hybrid, on-site). Recruiters filter by all three.

**Action:** Enable "Open to Work," select your visibility preference, and pin your resume PDF to the Featured section — do all three before closing the browser tab.

---

## Module Summary

LinkedIn is not a second resume. It is a searchable database that recruiters query by keyword. Every section — headline, About, Experience — needs to contain the same terms your resume uses, structured so the algorithm can index them and a human can read them. Optimize in this order: headline first, About second, Experience third, then hygiene. The first two changes alone will make a measurable difference in how often your profile appears in recruiter searches.[^6]
