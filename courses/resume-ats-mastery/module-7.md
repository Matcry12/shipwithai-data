# Module 7 (Capstone) — One Tailored Application Packet

**Objective:** Run the full pipeline on one real job, end to end.

This module is about assembling everything into a single, coherent application packet for one real role — and then submitting it. By the end, you will have an ATS-parseable resume, a tailored cover letter, an aligned LinkedIn profile, a submitted application, and a tracking row in place.

Work through each checkpoint in order. Do not skip ahead.

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Pick One Real Job

Open a job board right now and find one role you would actually accept. Not a placeholder. Not a practice target. A real posting at a company you would say yes to.

Save the full job description locally. You will return to it at every subsequent step.

The job description is your master reference. Every tailoring decision in this module flows from it.[^1]

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Extract Keywords from the Job Description

Read the posting line by line. Pull out:

- Required skills and tools (exact names, not paraphrases)
- The job title as written
- Action verbs used in the responsibilities section
- Any acronyms — note both the acronym and the full term

Target 10–20 keywords. For each one, note whether it already appears in your current resume.[^1]

When you list acronyms, include both forms. If the JD says "Search Engine Optimization (SEO)," your resume needs both phrases, not just the short version.[^2]

This list is your tailoring map. Keep it open while you work through the next three checkpoints.

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Tailor Your Resume

A one-size-fits-all resume rarely gets noticed in a competitive market. Customizing your resume to match the specific requirements of a posting significantly boosts the chances of getting through.

Work through these four steps:

1. **Update the headline.** Put the exact job title from the posting in your headline or summary. This is one of the clearest signals to both the ATS and the recruiter.
2. **Rewrite the summary.** Align it closely with the role. Use the JD's language, not synonyms.[^1]
3. **Inject keywords into experience bullets.** Match your experience to the job by highlighting the achievements most relevant to the role. Tailor means presenting your existing skills in a way that resonates with the employer — not fabricating qualifications.[^3]
4. **Update the Skills section.** Make sure the top keywords from your map appear here. Keywords should appear in multiple sections, not only Skills.[^2]

Name the file `FirstName_LastName_Resume.pdf`.[^4]

---

## Checkpoint 4 — Run the Plain-Text Test

Before you submit anything, confirm your resume will survive ATS parsing.

Copy all text from your resume and paste it into a plain text editor (Notepad on Windows, TextEdit on Mac). Read through the result carefully. If headings have shifted into the middle of content, characters look wrong, or sections are missing compared to the original, your formatting needs fixing before you proceed. Those same issues will occur when the ATS parses your document.[^5]

Aim for a 70–80% keyword match rate against the job description for required qualifications.[^6]

Run through this quick checklist before moving on[^7]:

- Single-column layout, reverse-chronological order
- Standard font (Arial, Calibri, or Roboto), 10–12pt body
- Margins 0.5–1 inch
- Standard section headers: Experience, Education, Skills
- Dates in MM/YYYY format, consistent throughout
- No graphics, skill bars, icons, or tables
- Contact info in the document body, not a header/footer
- File saved as PDF (or .docx if the employer requests it)
- Keywords matched to the target job description
- Both acronyms and full terms present

Do not proceed to the cover letter until the resume passes this checklist.

---

## Checkpoint 5 — Write the Cover Letter

Your resume proves you can do the job. Your cover letter proves you want this specific job, at this specific company, for the right reasons. They are a pair, not duplicates.[^8]

Structure:

1. **Header.** Your name, phone, email, and LinkedIn URL — in the same format as your resume header. This visual consistency signals attention to detail.[^8]
2. **Personalized greeting.** Address the hiring manager by name. Check LinkedIn or the company website. Generic greetings reduce response rates.[^9]
3. **Opening paragraph.** State the role and immediately connect it to a relevant, quantified achievement. Skip "I am writing to express my interest." Get to the point.
4. **Body.** One to two short paragraphs. Focus on the value you bring. Use the JD's language. Quantify. 70% of hiring managers prioritize quantifiable accomplishments over general skill claims.[^9]
5. **Closing.** Express genuine interest and invite next steps directly.

Keep it to one page. Save as PDF.

---

## Checkpoint 6 — Align LinkedIn

A recruiter who finds your resume may look up your LinkedIn profile within minutes. If the two documents contradict each other, you lose credibility.

Three things to align before you submit:

- **Headline.** It should reflect the same role keyword as your resume headline.
- **About section.** The opening hook should echo your resume summary. Keywords from the JD you wove into the resume belong here too.
- **Experience entries.** Job titles, employer names, and dates must match your resume exactly. The achievement bullets do not need to be identical, but they should be consistent.

Make sure your profile is set to "Open to Work" and your custom URL is active.[^10]

---

## Checkpoint 7 — Final Pre-Submission Check

Before you hit submit, run through these in order:

- Resume passes the plain-text test (Checkpoint 4)
- The exact job title from the posting appears in your resume headline
- Your top 10 JD keywords appear naturally in the resume
- Every experience bullet uses a strong action verb and includes a quantified result where possible
- Cover letter header matches resume header
- Cover letter is addressed to a named person, not a generic greeting
- LinkedIn headline, summary, and experience are consistent with your resume
- File names follow `FirstName_LastName_Resume.pdf` format
- Proofread for typos — spellcheck alone misses errors; manual review is required[^11]

---

## Checkpoint 8 — Submit and Track

Submit the application. Then immediately create a tracking row for it.

Track, at minimum: company name, job title, date applied, application URL, and current status. Tracking your applications keeps you organized and ensures no opportunity slips through the cracks.[^10]

If you do not hear back, send one follow-up. Wait 5–7 business days after applying. Send Tuesday through Thursday, 10–11 AM in the hiring manager's time zone. Keep it short: reference the role, note one specific reason you are a strong fit, and express continued interest. One follow-up only — then focus your energy elsewhere.[^12]

The tracking row is your feedback loop. After ten applications you will see patterns: which job titles get responses, which resume versions perform better. That data tells you what to adjust.

---

**Capstone deliverable:** one complete job application packet — an ATS-parseable, keyword-tailored resume; a one-page cover letter with a named greeting and at least one quantified achievement; a LinkedIn profile aligned to the same role keyword and narrative — submitted to one real job posting, with a tracking row recorded.
