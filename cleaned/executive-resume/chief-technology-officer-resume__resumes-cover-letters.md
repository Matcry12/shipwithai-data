## What is a chief technology officer?
A CTO, or chief technology officer, is an information technology executive position within a company. The chief technology officer implements business strategies and guides other leaders within the company. They're leading experts and have extensive knowledge of both business practices and advanced technology. They thoroughly understand computer hardware, software and applications and can select technology that effectively supports business. A chief technology officer can work with companies in many industries to provide technical support, including the legal sector, manufacturing firms or marketing agencies.Read more: [What Is a CTO? ## Why is writing an effective chief technology officer resume important?
Writing an effective chief technology officer resume is important because it's a tool for communicating your value to a potential employer. The resume is likely the first information the hiring manager or hiring team reads about you, so earning and retaining their attention is vital. To write a CTO resume effectively, you succinctly communicate who you are, your technology expertise and how your addition can contribute to the success of their organization. Some hiring teams can review hundreds of resumes when trying to find their ideal chief technology officer, so creating an impactful resume is crucial.Read more: [Why Is a Resume Important?](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/why-is-a-resume-important)
## How to write a chief technology officer resume
You can follow these steps to write an effective chief technology officer resume:
### 1. Choose the best resume format for you
Before you add content to your resume, choose the best format for organizing and presenting your qualifications. The format that best suits your professional profile depends on your strengths. Regardless of how you choose to organize the information, your resume should be only a single page, use a simple font, have 1-inch margins and omit pictures and other unnecessary flourishes. A straightforward design can help you ensure your professional information is the most attractive component of your resume. These are some resume formats you may choose from:
  * Chronological resume format: The chronological resume format is one of the most common to use, and it may be best for you if you have a consistent work experience section. It lists your previous positions in reverse chronological order, showing where you've worked, for how long and the responsibilities you managed in each position.
  * Functional resume format: If you have gaps in your work history or are making a career change, the functional resume format may be the best option for you. It includes your objective statement and your relevant skills as the focus at the top of the page, allowing you to show how you are capable without relying on your experience.
  * Combination resume format: This format is best for candidates with moderate skills and experiences, as it uses features from both the chronological and functional resume formats. This structure allows you to focus on your experience and abilities, but you may need to remove additional sections or your objective statement.

Read more: [2021's Top Resume Formats: Tips and Examples of Three Common Resumes](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/resume-format-guide-with-examples)
### 2. Review the job listing details for keywords
Many hiring teams use resume-scanning software that identifies the best candidates for the position based on which resumes contain the same keywords as the job posting. Because of this, it's important to review the job listing and other information about the position and determine which words may be keywords in those systems. To do this, identify which words are present multiple times or priorities within the job listing. You can also review information about the company on their website or social media platforms, as this can help you understand their philosophies and craft a resume that aligns with them.
### 3. List your current contact information
At the top of the resume document, list your current contact information. This includes your full name, location, email address and phone number. For application materials, it's best to use a professional email solely for business correspondence. Your professional email is usually some combination of your first and last name at either your own website or a different email provider. If you don't have a professional email, consider obtaining one before submitting your application materials.
### 4. Craft a concise resume objective
Depending on the resume format you choose, your resume objective may be the first thing the hiring manager reads. The resume object introduces your abilities and career aspirations to your potential employer. It communicates who you are and how you can bring value to their organization, so it's important to use powerful language and evidence of your abilities. To do this, you can start by stating your professional title, your relevant experience and unique skills or achievements.Example: Innovative CTO with over 20 years of experience developing and implementing advanced technological solutions. Pursuing an executive position within an international organization.Read more: [What Is a Resume Objective? 42 Examples for Different Industries](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/career-development/objectives-examples)
### 5. Create your work experience section
When applying for a chief technology officer position, the hiring manager or hiring team is likely interested in your work experience. A CTO must be capable of leadership and have a thorough understanding of computer applications and technologies, and your work experience can show that. In your work experience section, include your professional positions, where they were, how long you worked there and the responsibilities you managed while in each position. Use present tense for the responsibilities in positions you currently hold and past tense for your previous positions.
### 6. Emphasize your education and degrees
Though it depends on the company and industry, you likely need at least a bachelor's degree to receive consideration for a chief technology executive position. If you want a position with a large or international company, you may need more advanced education. Create your education section and include your degrees, the institutions that issued them and your dates of attendance. You can also include additional information like any awards you earned or graduating honors.
### 7. Thoroughly edit and proofread
The last step in creating an effective resume is editing and proofreading. To edit your resume, review your language, structure and content. Ensure everything you've included has a purpose and make the most of the space by using impactful and concise language. Be specific but accurate when detailing your skills and achievements. To proofread, review your resume with a focus on spelling and grammar. You can ask your friends, family and colleagues for help in editing and proofreading your resume. You can also use online grammar and spell-checking programs to ensure your resume is as professional as possible.Related: [27 Proofreading Tips That Will Improve Your Resume](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/proofreading-tips-that-will-improve-your-resume)
