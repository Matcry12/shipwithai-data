A professional resume can be the deciding factor for a prospective employer looking at your job application. A well-written resume displays professionalism, confidence and your most important skills and credentials as an engineering vice president (VP). Understanding the steps to write a good VP engineering resume can help you create a great supplement for your next job application. In this article, we show you what a resume is, how to write one and explore a template and resume example for reference.

## What is a VP of engineering resume?
A VP of engineering resume is a document that highlights your professional accomplishments, credentials, specialized skills , work experience and education. This document is part of the application process for many employers, as it summarizes each potential candidate and expands on the key hiring factors that employers are seeking.For example, a VP of engineering candidate can highlight their leadership experience, which might make them a more suitable choice for a VP position. A resume is typically one page long and contains only relevant information about the position you're seeking. A VP of engineering might exclude their experience as a retail sales associate to keep a resume focused on their engineer experience instead.Related: [Writing an Effective Software Engineering Internship Resume](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/software-engineering-internship-resume)

## How to write a VP of engineering resume
Use these six easy steps when writing your VP of engineering resume:
### 1. Outline your resume
Outline your resume using an online template. You can also consider where you want to put your information on the page. An outline can help you better organize your resume and make it look more professional. Choose an outline that highlights your most important skills and credentials. For example, you might choose a format with a more focused skills section so you can list your expertise in engineering principles.Outlining is also a good chance to review all of your personal information and experience relating to the job description. A good resume typically matches the job description of the position you're seeking, which helps increase your visibility to potential employers.Related: [Top 30 Keywords to List on a Software Engineer Resume](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/software-engineering-resume-keywords)
### 2. Write a professional summary or introduction
It's important to introduce yourself to your prospective employer with a short professional summary. This tells the employer who you are, what qualifications you have for the job and what engineering and leadership goals you have for the future. A professional summary can be two to five sentences but ensure that it's concise and includes your name and title. For example, you could write :Professional, experienced software engineering VP with over 10 years in the industry. Proficient with the software engineering process, coding and development and product management. Seeking a fast-paced, innovative company to work for where I can expand my engineering skills through collaborative projects.
### 3. Create a professional header
A professional resume includes a header at the top of the page, which displays personal contact information. The header can include your full name, email address, phone number and a link to your professional online portfolio, career website profile or professional social media page. It's important to review your contact information to ensure it's current and accurate, as this is what your prospective employer uses to contact you for an interview or job offer.Try to use a professional email address from school, work or a personal email address that includes your name. Your portfolio and social media links should direct readers to professional pages free of personal views and inappropriate content. For example, you might provide your employer with a career website profile that includes your resume instead of your personal social media pages.
### 4. Highlight your engineering skills
Highlight your specific engineering, leadership and personal soft skills that you think make you a suitable candidate for the job. You can use a bulleted list for readability, briefly explaining each skill. You can also include only the skills in a list and discuss them more when you get an interview. It's important to display your most important skills on your resume so the reader immediately understands the value you can bring to the company. For example, you might include software engineering skills or drafting skills along with leadership skills, like coaching and communication.Read more: [12 Essential Engineering Skills for Your Resume](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/essential-engineering-skills)
### 5. Include your work history in engineering
Include your professional work history, starting with your most recent or current engineering or leadership position. A resume typically only includes the work history that applies to the position you're seeking, so you can omit anything that isn't engineering-related. If you're applying for a VP of engineering position, you might have several years of experience in both leadership and engineering. List your professional title at each job, the name of the company and how long you worked there, including the month and year.You can also include a bulleted list of your core responsibilities or accomplishments with the company. A bulleted list can be easier to read and allows you to expand on specific ideas or accomplishments during the interview. Remember that your resume is simply a professional summary of your work history, skills and education.
### 6. Display your engineering credentials and educational accomplishments
Display your engineering credentials and education at the bottom of your resume after your work history. This section can be concise, including only minimal information about your education. For example, you can include the name of the certification or diploma, followed by the city, state and name of the educational institution you attended and the year you graduated. If an employer wants to know more about your credentials or how you apply them on the job, you can answer those questions during your interview or review process.Read more: [Tips for Writing an Engineering Resume Objective (With Examples)](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/engineering-resume-objective)
