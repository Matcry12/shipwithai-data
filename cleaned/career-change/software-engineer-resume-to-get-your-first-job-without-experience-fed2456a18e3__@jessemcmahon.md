Hey everyone! I figured this would be a very good topic to write about because no matter what programming language you're learning, you are going to need a great resume to land that first job! In this article I will discuss tips and general guidelines to help you fill up your resume with quality information, even without any professional software engineering experience.

So as someone who did not go to school for computer science (I was a finance major) I was very anxious about people just throwing my resume in the garbage. Little did I know when I first started was that a lot of companies do not care if you have a CS degree. In fact, a lot of companies actually encourage non CS students to apply. This is due to the fact that having employees from all different walks of life will help bring different insights to all of the challenges the company faces on a daily basis. So here are some tips to help you really crush your resume!

First and foremost you have to have experience writing code. This doesn't have to be professional experience by any means but you do have to have some coding skills. My suggestion would be to start by taking courses on Udemy in the language you desire to learn. This is what I did and it was very cheap (under $20 a course) and very effective. I would encourage anyone just starting out to take Colt Steele's Web Developer Bootcamp on Udemy as their first course. This was the course that I took and it was amazing! I would also strongly encourage everyone's first language to be Javascript. I say this because it is the most in-demand in terms of job openings and there is SO MUCH information on any issues you may have so you'll never been stuck unable to find an answer (even if it may seem like that sometimes!).

After you have taken a few courses you must start working on personal projects. It doesn't have to be the next Facebook or anything but you need to showcase your skills to potential employers. Start out simple and gradually add new features to future projects (See my last article "Learn to code quickly: One step at a time" for more on this). Once you have 3–4 REALLY solid projects I would say you are ready to start applying. You don't want to wait too long to start applying especially since interviews are hard and every single one you get is great practice for the next one.

So once you feel you are ready to start applying you need to build your resume. I used Resume.io to build my website and I would strongly encourage others to do the same. They have plenty of templates to choose from and the way you build your resume is very user friendly so you don't have to worry about styling or anything like that. Whether you use a templating website or not just remember to ***keep it simple.*** Pick a nice modern design and make sure when you look at it that you can understand it's message immediately. If you can't it's safe to assume that nobody else can either.

Once you have your template picked out it's time to start adding your experience. Now if you went to school for Computer Science, great put it at the top of your resume. If not and you have a Bachelors degree in anything else that's awesome too, but your actual skills and projects should go above it. So if you do not have a CS degree, the first thing (besides your name, address, email, etc.) at the top of the page should be a "Profile". A profile is just a short 3–4 sentence summary of how you got into coding and why you want to work as a Software Engineer. Make sure it is captivating. A lot of recruiters might not even read it but if they do, you want them to be intrigued and check out the rest of the resume. Then after that, depending on the resume template you use, you want to list your skills. I personally use a resume that has all of my skills/relevant links listed on the side but that's just my preference. Now skills are technologies you have experience with. Examples of this could be HTML, CSS, Javascript, React, Redux, Angular, Git, NodeJS, Python, etc.. The list goes on forever but those are some of the most in demand technologies right now. Remember anything that you put on your resume is fair game for a recruiter to ask you about so only put things that you *really* understand on there.

After skills should come your projects. Structure these like this: The name of the project, a link to where the project is hosted or the github repository, a short 2–3 sentence description of what the project is, and then what technologies you used to make the project. Every resume should be submitted as a PDF file so that recruiters can click on any links that you include. After listing all of your projects you want to list your education history if it is not Computer Science related. Just write this part like you would any other type of resume, nothing fancy.

Last but definitely not least, list any leadership experience you might have. Whether it is sports, student government, clubs, anything where you were in charge of or lead others. Companies look for people that can make others around them better so any experience doing that in any capacity is great.
