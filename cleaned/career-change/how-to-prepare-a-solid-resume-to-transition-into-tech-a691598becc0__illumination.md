When applying for my first full-time position in Corporate America, I was lost on where and how to start. My university had a Career Center; I was able to gather resources, attend events about applying for jobs, and actually do a *mock* interview with Mastercard. It was the first time I learned what a mock interview was.

I learned so much about approaching these interviews and positioning myself when my time to enter the workforce would arrive.

The more you walk your professional journey, the more you learn. So, when I was applying for jobs, I realized some positions would require different resumes, different objectives highlighted in these resumes, and also different templates.

By that time, my professional experience was focused on Brazil's market, more specifically in the Marketing space.

Depending on the role or the company, I could be more creative on my resume, more artistic, or more serious.

It was tough to find good templates, and it took me a long time to create my own system of job application: different resumes for different job positions, different cover letters for different industries, Google Sheets to keep track of all my applications, and so on. Learning all the ins and outs of applying for jobs in Corporate America took time, and I wish someone had taught me that a long time ago.

Below, I will share some tips I wish I had known when I started my career in technology in the United States.

#### Highlight relevant technology skills

Avoid general Skills like "Microsoft Word" is not a relevant technical skill. For technical roles, be specific with your programming languages skills and certifications. Include your proficiency in each one of the programming languages.

#### Transferable skills

Transferable skills are the ones you can transfer from one role in an industry to another in a completely different one. Some examples are leadership, communication skills, sales, negotiation, creating and preparing presentations, and public speaking.

#### Volunteer Work

I can't stress enough how important it is to highlight your volunteer work. The extracurricular courses you participate in. Recruiters are very interested in learning how you overcome problems, your passion for a different subjects, and how you handle situations with different groups and peers.

Having all these in your "toolbox" will make you a different and unique candidate. Be yourself and bring your authentic self to the interview. That's what I did when I was telling my story about moving from [Marketing to Computer Science](https://medium.com/illumination/from-marketing-to-computer-science-how-i-made-my-career-transition-7e88c59e3b34).
