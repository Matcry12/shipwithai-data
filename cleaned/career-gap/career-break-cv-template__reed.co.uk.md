# Career break CV template

**Looking for a new job after a lengthy career break and not sure where to start?**

Whether your career break was due to travel, redundancy, further study, personal illness, family commitments, or something else – knowing how to address it on your CV can be a challenge. But the biggest mistake you can make is to ignore it.

The example we've used in this template is for a break brought on by family commitments, but for more specific help, you can read our guide on how to explain a gap in your CV.

**What is a career break?**

A career break is an extended period away from your usual employment, typically lasting several months or years. Career break reasons can include:

**Family commitments**– raising children, caring for elderly relatives**Health and wellbeing**– recovery from illness, mental health support**Education and development**– further study, professional qualifications**Personal growth**– travel, volunteering, pursuing passions**Life transitions**– relocation, redundancy, career changes

Taking a sabbatical year or extended career break is increasingly common, with many employers recognising the value of well-rounded candidates who bring diverse life experiences to their roles.

**Career break CV template structure**

When creating your career break CV template, follow this proven structure:

**Contact details****Personal statement****Skills****Employment history****Education****Hobbies and interests**(optional)**References**

**How to write your CV after a career break**

Ready to turn your time away into your next big selling point? Here's how to reshape your CV and show employers you're more than prepared for a strong comeback.

**Contact details**

Keep it simple and professional. Include your:

- Full name
- Location (city and postcode)
- Phone number
- Email address
- LinkedIn profile (if you have one)

**Personal statement**

Your personal statement is crucial when returning from a career break. This 3-4 sentence summary should answer three key questions:

**Who are you?**Briefly describe your professional background**What do you offer?**Highlight your key skills and experience**What are your aims?**Express your enthusiasm for returning to work

Mention your career break briefly (just one line) and focus on your eagerness to re-enter the workforce. Save detailed explanations for your cover letter.

Example personal statement:

Experienced marketing professional with five years' experience in digital campaigns and brand management. Recently completed a career break to focus on family commitments, during which I maintained industry knowledge through online courses and freelance projects. Now seeking to return to a marketing role where I can apply my strategic thinking and creative skills to drive business growth.

**Skills**

This section is particularly important for career break CVs. Lead with skills that remain relevant, regardless of when you last used them professionally. Focus on:

**Hard skills:**

- Software proficiency (Microsoft Office, Adobe Creative Suite, etc.)
- Technical abilities
- Industry-specific knowledge
- Languages

**Soft skills:**

- Communication
- Problem-solving
- Project management
- Leadership

Provide specific examples where possible. If you've maintained or developed skills during your break through volunteering, courses, or personal projects, mention these.

**Employment history**

List your work experience in reverse chronological order. For your career break, you have two options:

**Option 1: Brief explanation of your career break**

Include a simple line such as:

'Career break – family commitments (2020–2023)' or 'Sabbatical – travel and personal development (2021–2022)'.

While not essential, you can choose to add more detail about any relevant skills, volunteering, personal projects, or courses you completed during this time – but only if it's relevant to the job you're applying for.

Adding this detail is particularly useful for people who don't have any recent work experience to note.

**Option 2: Focus on productive activities instead of labelling it as a career break**

If you spent your break doing freelance work, volunteering, or gaining new qualifications, list these as roles in your employment history.

For example:

Freelance Marketing Consultant(2021–2023)– Provided digital marketing support to three local businesses

- Completed Google Analytics certification
- Managed social media accounts, increasing engagement by 25%Completed Google Analytics certification

For employment before your break, focus on achievements rather than just duties. Use numbers and specific examples to demonstrate your impact. This means using the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result) to describe your achievements, even from unpaid work.

**Education**

Include your qualifications from GCSE level onwards in reverse chronological order.

If you've completed any courses or gained certifications during your career break, include these prominently. They show you've used your time productively and stayed current in your field.

Example:

- Google Analytics Certified (2022)
- Project Management Course, Coursera (2021)
- BA (Hons) Business Studies, University of Manchester (2005-2008), 2:1

**Hobbies and interests**

This optional section can be valuable for career break CVs as it helps fill potential gaps and shows you as a well-rounded person. Only include interests that:

- Demonstrate relevant skills
- Show personal development
- Are genuinely interesting to discuss in interviews

Avoid generic entries like 'socialising with friends' – instead, opt for specific activities that showcase desirable qualities.

**References**

Simply state 'References available upon request'. Prepare a separate list of referees, including:

- Former managers or colleagues
- Academic contacts
- Professional mentors
- Volunteer coordinators (if applicable)

A reference from a previous employer can be particularly valuable, showing you left on good terms and maintained professional relationships.

**Career break CV examples**

The following examples show how you can address a career break within the Work Experience section of your CV. These snippets demonstrate how to explain a gap clearly, while focusing on skills gained and readiness to return to work.

**Example 1: Family career break**

**Career Break**

*2019–2023*

Took time away from finance roles to focus on family commitments. During this period, completed AAT Level 4 qualification and maintained up-to-date knowledge of industry trends. Now ready to apply refreshed skills in a new finance position.

**Example 2: Travel career break**

**Career Break – Travel and Volunteering**

*2022–2023*

Travelled throughout Southeast Asia, developing cultural awareness and conversational language skills. Volunteered with local charities, improving communication skills and adaptability. Keen to return to sales management with a worldwide perspective.

**Example 3: Further study career break**

**Career Break – Further Education**

*2022*

Completed a full-time Digital Marketing Diploma, with a focus on SEO, analytics, and campaign strategy. Used this time to build new technical skills and keep knowledge current, ready to contribute fresh ideas to a marketing team.

Remember, CVs should be succinct (no longer than two pages). If you're looking to provide more detailed context, it's best to do it in your cover letter and/or at the interview.

**Tips for getting back into the job market after a career break**

Once your CV is polished, here are some practical steps for your job search:

**Network****actively**– reconnect with former colleagues and industry contacts**Consider****temporary work**– it can be a good way to ease back into employment**Attend industry events**– shows you're serious about your return**Update your LinkedIn**– ensure it matches your CV and shows you're actively job hunting**Prepare for interviews**– be ready to discuss your career break confidently and positively

**Final thoughts**

Remember, taking a career break shows you're someone who makes thoughtful life decisions and isn't afraid to prioritise what matters most – and these are valuable qualities that many employers appreciate.

Your career break doesn't define your professional worth – your skills, experience, and enthusiasm for getting back to work do. With a well-crafted CV that presents your career break positively and highlights your continued value as an employee, you'll be well on your way to landing a job you'll love.

The most important thing is to approach your job search with confidence. You've got this.
