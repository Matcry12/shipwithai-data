# 4 things you NEED in your GitHub Cloud Developer Portfolio


### **If you’re a developer, or aspiring to become one, you’ve probably already heard of GitHub. The web-based interface can host code projects while giving developers the opportunity to collaborate and network with like-minded professionals. **
### **Other GitHub users, currently standing at more than 56 million, can check out your code and propose any changes. All of this comes at zero cost, as users can enjoy this platform for free, making it increasingly popular within the industry, especially when it comes to hosting developer portfolios. **
### **And,[ with cloud computing being one of the fastest-growing professions,](https://www.crn.com/slide-shows/cloud/the-most-in-demand-cloud-computing-jobs-for-2021) there’s never been a better time to showcase and make sure that your GitHub portfolio is employer-ready. **
## First thing's first... do you really need a Developer Portfolio?
Simply put, yes. A comprehensive developer portfolio and a compelling resume go together like bread and butter. In such a competitive industry, finding ways of setting yourself apart from the competition is a must to top the list and land your dream job.
Undeniably, having an exhaustive portfolio that includes all the projects you’ve previously tackled and worked on will set you up for success.
If done right, portfolios can give hiring managers an insight into what you can do as a developer, serve as proof that you really do have the skills included on your resume, and reveal soft skills such as problem-solving, logic, and creative thinking.
And, thanks to the collaborative nature of GitHub, it can even reveal your teamwork or project management skills.
## Okay, so what should I include in my portfolio?
Your portfolio is where you can demonstrate your programming skills—showing not only what you can do, but also how. It can also give you a competitive edge over the competition, whether you’re just starting out in your career, or progressing within it. So, including the right information is key—and the following are just some of the essentials you should include in your GitHub Developer Portfolio.
## GitHub Portfolio essential #1: An optimized profile
So, you’ve [created your GitHub account](https://github.com/join). It’s now time to optimize it. The more enhanced your account is, the higher the probability of your profile being found and noticed by hiring managers or recruiters. 
Let’s start with your **about** section. This is your opportunity to introduce yourself, so make sure that this section includes your real name, your contact details (such as your email, as well as any social media profiles relevant to your role). 
Another crucial element is your **bio** —make the most of the allotted 160 characters to write an optimized and informative introduction, one that introduces you in the best light possible.
When doing so, keep your audience and context in mind. While recruiters and hiring managers might be looking at your GitHub profile, they’re probably not expecting the same formal tone or setup as on your resume or cover letter, or even LinkedIn profile. So, make it concise, optimized, and fun! 
## GitHub Portfolio essential #2: Assignments or coursework
Particularly for fresh graduates, putting together a portfolio can sound like a daunting task—as finding the right work to include might be tricky. If you’re looking at entry-level jobs, including any work you’ve done as part of your university coursework or assignments is a good starting point.
To set yourself further apart, use these projects as a foundation upon which you can build larger projects—helping you to stand out without having to start a project from scratch!
## GitHub Portfolio essential #3: Programming projects
One other way of showcasing your professional skills, as well as your areas of expertise, is through personal projects. Not only will these illustrate how good you are as a developert—they’re also a great way to demonstrate any causes you’re passionate about.
Especially if you’re looking to apply within a certain field or technology you don’t have any previous experience in—personal projects can be a way of tailoring your knowledge and skills to a particular position or sector.
Say you’re eyeing a developer role in the finance industry—building a portfolio based on projects that focus on providing solutions to common problems within the finance industry is more likely to grab the attention of a recruiter or hiring manager. Not only does it demonstrate your experience, but also passion, interest, and ecosystem knowledge—which, is priceless in such a competitive industry.
## GitHub Portfolio essential #4: Personal projects
Anything that helps strengthen your skills and credibility within the industry, as well as showcases your skills and knowledge belongs in your portfolio.
So, if you write a blog, or use social media platforms to create content related to your profession, make sure you include that on your GitHub portfolio as well.
Doing so will not only demonstrate your industry knowledge and show that you’re up-to-date with the latest news. It will also showcase your willingness to learn, which is key in a rapidly-evolving industry such as tech.
This is also likely to demonstrate your ability to network within the industry—by their nature, blogs and social media accounts thrive on interaction, and that is definitely a plus for employability!
## In summary
GitHub can seriously change the game when it comes to your developer portfolio. It’s free, easy to use, and can be personalized in different ways to show off both your skills and personality. It’s also a goldmine for learning opportunities—you can collaborate with other GitHub users and contribute to their projects, and that is great to have on your profile.
And, as time goes by and you work on more projects and gain more experience, don’t forget to update your portfolio regularly. You never know when the right opportunity will come knocking, so it’s always best to be prepared!
