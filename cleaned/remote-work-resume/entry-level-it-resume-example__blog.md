**This is how to write a job-winning entry-level IT resume:**
## 1. Use the Best Format for Your Entry-Level IT Resume Template
People in IT work in companies or as freelancers to provide technical and digital support in hardware, software, apps, the internet, and cybersecurity. The purpose of your entry-level IT resume is to show that you have the experience and skills needed to support and create all the different aspects of today’s high tech world.
IT covers a massive amount of different specializations. IT employees can be found both in positions from data analysis and software developers to project managers and directors. The type of work they do also varies with IT employees working both in company and as freelancers. Obviously, each IT position requires its own individual set of skills.
Job growth for computer and information technology is [projected to grow](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/computer-and-information-technology/home.htm) more than average for the coming years. That’s a double-edged sword since more growth means more candidates wanting a piece of that pie.
To get the recruiter to download your profile, you should follow these [resume formatting rules](https://zety.com/blog/resume-formats):
  * Check that your [resume contact information](https://zety.com/blog/resume-contact-information) is current. Also make sure[ your social media footprint](https://zety.com/blog/check-your-online-presence) doesn’t contain anything employers might find unprofessional. 
  * [Optimizing your LinkedIn profile](https://zety.com/blog/optimize-your-linkedin-profile) is a great hack. Use it as a tool that can share more information about your job experience and overall interest in IT through profile sections or articles you share. 
  * Out of all [resume styles](https://zety.com/blog/resume-styles), the [chronological layout](https://zety.com/blog/chronological-resume) works best with entry-level IT resumes. It really underlines your professional experience and IT skills. Plus it’s the format that most recruiters recognize and it makes your [resume more ATS friendly](https://zety.com/blog/ats-resume).
  * Pick a [resume font](https://zety.com/blog/best-fonts-for-resume) that’s easy to read and professional. Just imagine looking at code written in Comic Sans or cursive and you’ll get the idea why it’s important. Go with a 12 pt size and leave a little white space to make it easier on the eyes.
  * Unless you want your [resume layout](https://zety.com/blog/resume-layout) looking like a coding disaster, save it in a PDF format. 


You’re probably asking yourself the neverending question of [how long your resume should be](https://zety.com/blog/how-long-should-a-resume-be). The general rule is that a [one-page resume template](https://zety.com/resume-templates/one-page) is standard, unless you’ve been in IT since DOS was king.

## 2. Write an Entry-Level IT Resume Objective
Start off with your [resume profile](https://zety.com/blog/resume-profile) (summary or objective). It’s a short bit of coding that begins your entry-level IT resume and shows hiring managers that you’re not just another corrupted application.
Since you’re writing an [entry-level resume](https://zety.com/blog/entry-level-resume-example), go for the [career objective](https://zety.com/blog/resume-objective):
  * Underline knowledge and skills you’ve gained and include some good points on why you’d be a good fit for the company.
  * Mention relevant transferable skills and quantifiable achievements from other gigs you’ve had and school if they can prove your IT skills or know how.


Don’t go over 3-4 sentences long. Remember to [tailor it to the job description](https://zety.com/blog/tailor-your-resume) just like you choose the right code for the right project.
Read more: [**_How to Start a Resume_**](https://zety.com/blog/how-to-start-a-resume)
## 3. Make Your Entry-Level IT Resume Education Section Shine
You’re not going to go far in IT with the proper schooling. Here’s how to get your education section to make recruiters commit you to virtual memory:
  * Include: college/university name and location, years you studied, degree, [major and minors](https://zety.com/blog/how-to-list-minor-on-resume).
  * Enhance your [education resume section](https://zety.com/blog/education-on-resume) with college day wins (for example, your killer [GPA](https://zety.com/blog/gpa-on-resume) or [Latin honors](https://zety.com/blog/cum-laude-on-resume)). Just add what’s relevant and no more than 4-6 items.


Since you’re writing an entry-level IT resume with no experience, consider putting your education section _above_ your work experience. It’ll help you focus on your strengths which is a great way to start off your [resume with no experience](https://zety.com/blog/resume-with-no-work-experience).
Read more: [**_How to List a Degree on a Resume_**](https://zety.com/blog/how-to-list-degree-on-resume)
## 4. Create the Perfect Entry-Level IT Resume Job Description
If you have some professional experience, definitely include a [resume experience section](https://zety.com/blog/work-experience-resume). But it needs to be more appealing than a 1 TB transfer speed, so use the following guidelines to format it properly:
  * Start from the job you have now and then go back in time.
  * Make each entry easy to understand. Include: [job titles](https://zety.com/blog/job-titles), company name, dates of employment, and 6 bullet points max with your achievements.
  * Use fewer bullet points with your earlier jobs. A max of 3 should do the trick.
  * Begin each bullet point with an [action verb](https://zety.com/blog/resume-action-words).
  * Use quantifiable achievements and numbers to impress. Just adding a laundry list of responsibilities is as great as a DDos.
  * Tailor your IT experience section to the open position so you don’t look like spam.

## 5. Highlight Your IT Skills
Skills are key, plain and simple. Despite that fact, recent studies show that only around [30% of IT professionals](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.procs.2017.11.342) will have the skills employers are looking for. 
Here’s how to list your IT skills that’ll have hiring managers pinging you right away:
  * Jot down the [job skills](https://zety.com/blog/what-skills-to-put-on-a-resume) you have—this means [soft skills and hard skills](https://zety.com/blog/hard-skills-soft-skills), [technical skills](https://zety.com/blog/technical-skills), [computer skills](https://zety.com/blog/computer-skills-for-resume), and any other relevant skills you have on your hard drive. 
  * Use the job ad as a guide for which skills you’ll want to highlight. Include them in your information technology entry-level resume. Make sure the skills you mention can be proved by your experience and education sections.

## 6. Add Extra Sections to Your Entry-Level IT Resume
You’ve got the motherboard of your resume down. Now it’s time to add in some Easter eggs in plain sight. 
  * Include foreign [language skills](https://zety.com/blog/resume-language-skills) that can be relevant to your role. It’s a global market out there and IT professionals often work with or in international teams. Knowing a foreign language just makes you easier to interact with.
  * Add [volunteering](https://zety.com/blog/volunteer-work-on-a-resume), [achievements and awards](https://zety.com/blog/achievements-for-resume), [projects](https://zety.com/blog/projects-on-resume), and even [hobbies and interests](https://zety.com/blog/hobbies-and-interests-on-a-resume) if they’re tied to IT or prove a skillset or expertise. Things like GitHub can really beef up your resume with examples of what you can do.
  * Mention IT [certifications](https://zety.com/blog/certifications-on-resume) if you have any. These become necessary higher up in cybersecurity, technical support, or as a computer technician so might as well get started now.

## 7. Attach a Cover Letter to Your Entry-Level IT Resume
Whether you [should include a cover letter](https://zety.com/blog/are-cover-letters-necessary) is a non-issue. Add one even if the job ad doesn’t ask for it. Recruiters read them and you can prove you’re hot stuff through extra examples.
Write an entry-level IT cover letter that will make you look like anything but a debbie:
  * Use a modern [cover letter format](https://zety.com/blog/cover-letter-format) that shows your professional side.
  * [Start your cover letter](https://zety.com/blog/how-to-start-a-cover-letter) with a “hook” that will incite the hiring manager to read on.
  * Include specific examples of your tech expertise to show that you can do the job from day 1.
  * Add an impactful call to action to [end your cover letter](https://zety.com/blog/how-to-end-a-cover-letter) with a bang.


[The ideal length of an effective cover letter](https://zety.com/blog/how-long-should-a-cover-letter-be) is around one page so don’t end up writing a C++ manual.
