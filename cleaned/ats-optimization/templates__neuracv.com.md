## Choosing the best resume template in 2026
The best template is the one recruiters and ATS parsers can read without fighting your layout. In 2026 that usually means a clear single-column or lightly structured design, conventional headings, and bullets that mention tools and outcomes from the job description.

## What is an ATS-friendly template?
An ATS-friendly template is a layout that keeps your story in a predictable order so applicant tracking software can read your headings, dates, and bullets without tripping over heavy design. It does not guarantee a job. It does make it more likely your file lands in front of a person when the employer uses parsing tools. NeuraCV labels those layouts clearly so you can pair them with a [free ATS check](https://neuracv.com/resources/resume-checker) before you submit.
For a deeper walkthrough on wording and structure, read our [how to write an ATS resume](https://neuracv.com/category/neuracv_blog/tutorials/how-to-write-ats-resume) guide and the explainer on [what an ATS is](https://neuracv.com/category/neuracv_blog/tutorials/what-is-ats).
## What makes a layout easier for ATS reads
  * Use conventional section titles such as Experience, Education, and Skills when you can.
  * Keep tables, text boxes, and icon strips light so content does not jump out of reading order.
  * Mirror important phrases from the job description naturally, not as a block of pasted keywords.
  * Export a test PDF or Word file and open it in the ATS checker when you change layout or font.
  * If the listing asks for a specific file type, follow that instruction before anything else.


## ATS parsing and why layout still matters
Applicant tracking systems like predictable section titles, bullets that survive export, and fonts that do not turn into boxes when you save as PDF. Our ATS-styled templates keep sidebars and icons under control so your skills and job titles stay in the right columns after upload.
Need something bolder for a portfolio review? Use the creative tab for that conversation. When you apply through a strict corporate portal, switch back to an ATS-first layout, run a quick export test, and match section names to the job description without stuffing keywords.
## Who these layouts fit best
New graduates often like the entry-level cluster because shorter sections and generous spacing read well on a phone. Finance and consulting roles gravitate toward layouts with restrained type. Tech candidates frequently pick clusters with room for stack lists and impact bullets. We adjust labels when hiring feedback tells us a bucket feels off, not when a trend chart says so.
Changing careers? Duplicate your document in the global ATS area, try a creative variant for networking, and keep the conservative file for online forms. Every preview still opens in the same NeuraCV editor, so you are not retyping the same story in three places.
