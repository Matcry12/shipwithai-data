### Common Mistakes to Avoid

* **Listing technologies without context** — Show what you built, not just what you know* **Generic bullet points** — Replace "Responsible for building features" with specific metrics and outcomes* **Missing quantifiable results** — Always add numbers: users served, performance gains, cost savings* **Overloading with buzzwords** — Use keywords naturally, not in random keyword dumps* **Inconsistent date formats** — Stick to one format (MM/YYYY or Month YYYY) throughout* **Ignoring soft skills** — Collaboration and communication matter as much as coding ability* **Two-page resume for entry-level roles** — Keep it to one page until you have 5+ years experience* **PDF compatibility issues** — Test your resume in ATS checkers before submitting

### FAQ

**How long should a Full-Stack Developer resume be?** One page for 0–5 years experience, two pages for senior roles. Recruiters scan quickly, so prioritize recent, relevant work.

**Should I include personal projects on my resume?** Yes, if they demonstrate skills relevant to the job. Link to live demos or GitHub repos. Treat them like professional experience with metrics and outcomes.

**What's the best format for an ATS-friendly resume?** Use standard sections (Summary, Skills, Experience, Education). Avoid tables, text boxes, headers/footers, and unusual fonts. Stick to .docx or plain-text PDF.

**Do I need to list every technology I've ever used?** No. Focus on technologies mentioned in the job description and tools you've used in the last 2–3 years. Quality beats quantity.

**How do I show full-stack skills without seeming scattered?** Group projects by outcome rather than technology. Show how you solved problems end-to-end, from database to UI, rather than listing disconnected front-end and back-end tasks.

**Should I include a cover letter with my resume?** Only if required. Most ATS systems prioritize resume parsing. If you write one, keep it under 250 words and focus on why you want this specific role.
