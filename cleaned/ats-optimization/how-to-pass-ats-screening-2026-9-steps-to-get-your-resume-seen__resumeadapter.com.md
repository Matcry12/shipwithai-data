## What Is ATS Screening?

ATS screening is the automated process where Applicant Tracking System software scans, parses, and ranks your resume based on how well it matches a specific job description. The ATS looks for:

**Keywords and phrases**that match the job posting**Formatting compatibility**so it can read and parse your content**Required qualifications**like certifications, degrees, and years of experience**Section structure**to categorize your skills, experience, and education

When your resume passes ATS screening, it moves to a recruiter's queue. When it does not, it sits in a digital black hole with no notification sent to you.

The most common ATS platforms used by employers include **Workday, Greenhouse, Lever, iCIMS, Taleo, and BambooHR**. Each parses resumes slightly differently, but the core principles for passing them are the same — and a free ATS resume scanner simulates how each of these parsers reads your document.

## Why Most Resumes Fail ATS Screening

Most job seekers do not realize their resume is being read by software before a human. Here are the top reasons resumes get filtered out:

### 1. Missing Keywords

The number one reason for ATS rejection is missing the exact keywords from the job description. If the posting says "project management" and your resume says "managed projects," some ATS systems will not make the connection.

### 2. Formatting Problems

Fancy templates with columns, tables, text boxes, headers, footers, and graphics break ATS parsing. The system cannot read text embedded in images or extract data from complex layouts.

### 3. Wrong File Format

Submitting your resume as a .jpg, .png, or image-based PDF means ATS sees a blank page. Always use .docx or a text-based .pdf.

### 4. Non-Standard Section Headers

Creative section names like "My Journey" instead of "Work Experience" or "What I Know" instead of "Skills" confuse ATS parsers. They rely on standard labels to categorize your information.

### 5. Keyword Stuffing Without Context

Some candidates hide keywords in white text or list dozens of skills without context. Modern ATS systems detect this, and recruiters will reject resumes that feel spammy.

### Step 2: Use a Clean, Single-Column Layout

Strip your resume down to a simple, top-to-bottom format:

**One column only**(no sidebars)**No tables, text boxes, or graphics****Standard fonts**: Arial, Calibri, Times New Roman, or Helvetica**Font size**: 10-12pt for body, 14-16pt for your name**Margins**: 0.5 to 1 inch on all sides

This ensures every ATS on the market can read your resume without issues.

### Step 3: Match Keywords from the Job Description

Read the job posting carefully and identify:

**Hard skills**: Python, SQL, Salesforce, AutoCAD, etc.**Soft skills**: cross-functional collaboration, stakeholder management**Certifications**: PMP, CPA, AWS Certified, SHRM-CP**Industry terms**: agile methodology, GAAP compliance, regulatory reporting**Job title variations**: "Software Engineer" vs "Software Developer"

Add these keywords naturally throughout your resume. Do not just dump them in a skills section.

### Step 4: Use Standard Section Headers

Use these exact labels so ATS can categorize your information:

| Section | Use This | Do Not Use This |
|---|---|---|
| Summary | Professional Summary | About Me, My Story |
| Experience | Work Experience | Career History, My Journey |
| Skills | Skills, Technical Skills | What I Know, Toolbox |
| Education | Education | Academic Background |
| Certifications | Certifications | Credentials, Badges |

### Step 5: Optimize Your File Format

**Best choice**: .docx (highest ATS compatibility)**Also good**: Text-based .pdf (not scanned or image-based)**Avoid**: .jpg, .png, .pages, .odt, or any image format

If you are unsure whether your PDF is text-based, try selecting text in it. If you cannot highlight individual words, it is an image and ATS cannot read it.

### Step 6: Add Context to Every Keyword

ATS ranks resumes higher when keywords appear in context with measurable results. Do not just list skills. Show how you used them.

| Approach | Example |
|---|---|
Weak (keyword only) | "Project management, budgeting, stakeholder communication" |
Strong (keyword in context) | "Led cross-functional project management for a $2M product launch, managing budgets and stakeholder communication across 4 departments" |

### Step 7: Write a Tailored Professional Summary

Your summary is the first thing ATS parses. Make it count:

- Include the
**exact job title**from the posting - Add
**3-5 top keywords**from the job description - Keep it to
**3-4 sentences** - Include a
**measurable achievement**

**Example:**

"Results-driven Marketing Manager with 7+ years of experience in digital marketing, demand generation, and marketing analytics. Led campaigns that increased qualified leads by 45% and reduced cost-per-acquisition by 30%. Skilled in HubSpot, Google Analytics, and cross-functional team leadership."

### Step 8: Remove ATS-Breaking Elements

Delete these from your resume immediately:

**Headers and footers**(ATS often skips content in these areas)**Text boxes**(content inside text boxes is invisible to many ATS)**Embedded charts or infographics****Logos or icons**(including LinkedIn icons, phone icons, etc.)**Non-standard bullet characters**(use standard round bullets)**Columns created with tables**(use tab stops instead if needed)

### Step 9: Test Again Before You Submit

After making all changes, scan your resume one more time. Your score should have improved significantly.

**Is it better to submit a .docx or .pdf?**

Both work well with modern ATS systems. If you are unsure, .docx is the safest choice. Avoid image-based PDFs.

**Should I use the exact same words from the job description?**

Yes. ATS performs keyword matching, so using the exact terms from the job posting increases your score. Include both the full term and common abbreviations (e.g., "Search Engine Optimization (SEO)").

**Can I beat ATS with a one-page resume?**

Page length does not affect ATS scoring. ATS cares about keywords, formatting, and structure, not the number of pages. Use as many pages as you need to include relevant keywords and achievements.
