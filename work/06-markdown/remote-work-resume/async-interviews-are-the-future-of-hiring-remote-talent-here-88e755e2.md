Traditional resume screening takes too long and tells you too little. You're trying to identify qualified candidates from polished resumes, coordinating calendars across continents, and manually tracking everything. It doesn't scale, and it doesn't give you the insight you need to make confident hiring decisions.

Here's what companies are doing instead: they're replacing time-intensive resume review with async video screening that reveals actual candidate quality in a fraction of the time.

Let's get into it.

**What Are Asynchronous Interviews?**

Asynchronous interviews are pre-recorded responses where candidates answer your questions on their schedule, and you review them on yours.

You create questions in your platform, send the link to candidates, and review responses when it fits your calendar. Candidates get the link via email, record responses within 5-7 days, and submit when they're ready.

A candidate in Singapore records at 10 PM their time. You watch it at 9 AM in New York the next morning. The interview happens without anyone coordinating calendars.

**What async interviews replace in your process**

Async interviews replace the most time-intensive part of traditional hiring: initial screening.

**Old way:** Resume review → Phone screen → Live interview → Final interview

**New way:** Async interview (replaces resume review and phone screens) → Live interview → Final interview

Instead of spending hours reviewing resumes then conducting phone screens to verify basic qualifications, you watch candidates demonstrate their communication skills, articulate their experience, and walk through relevant scenarios. You get better information faster, then invest your time in meaningful conversations with candidates you know are qualified.

**Why this matters for your hiring process**

When you're dealing with high application volumes, resume screening becomes a bottleneck. You're trying to assess candidate quality from bullet points, which often leaves you guessing at who's worth talking to.

Async interviews give you richer information upfront. When candidates record responses, you see:

- How they communicate in real scenarios (not just what's written on paper)

- Whether they can articulate their experience clearly

- If they've thought through problems relevant to the role

- Their genuine interest and enthusiasm for the opportunity

This helps you make better decisions about who to advance, which means less time wasted on phone screens with people who aren't the right fit.

**Retake options:** 1-2 retakes per question so candidates can refine answers if needed.

**Multiple formats:** Video, audio-only (for candidates who prefer it), text answers, and file uploads for work samples.

**How Do Async and Live Interviews Work Together?**

They serve different purposes in your hiring funnel, and both are valuable.

Use async at the top of your funnel for efficient initial screening. Use live interviews in the middle and bottom to build relationships and assess fit with candidates you've already qualified.

Here's how it works for a customer success manager role:

**Stage 1 (Async):** Screen 150 applicants with 4 questions about conflict resolution and communication. Takes 3 days. You watch responses on your schedule, identify the top 15-20%, and make advancement decisions without conducting 150 phone screens.

**Stage 2 (Live):** 45-minute video interviews with the top 12 candidates. You already know they can communicate well and have relevant experience because you've seen them demonstrate it. Use this time to assess chemistry, answer their questions, and evaluate culture fit. Takes 1 week.

**Stage 3 (In-person):** Bring 3 finalists onsite for team meetings.

You use async to make your screening process more efficient, then invest your time in conversations with candidates you're confident about.

Async interviews solve this by giving you richer candidate information upfront, without the scheduling complexity of phone screens.

**Step 3: Write questions that reveal relevant capability**

Your questions determine whether async interviews give you useful information.

**Keep it focused.** 3-5 questions totaling 15-20 minutes. More than that and completion rates drop significantly.

**Ask questions that matter for the role.** Focus on skills and scenarios relevant to job success. You want to learn things resumes can't show you—how someone thinks, communicates, and approaches problems.

**Match time to complexity:**

30-60 seconds for straightforward questions ("What experience do you have with Salesforce? Walk me through how you've used it.")

1-2 minutes for behavioral questions ("Tell me about a time you had to change your approach on a project. What happened and what did you do?")

2-3 minutes for complex scenarios ("Walk me through how you'd handle this customer situation from start to finish.")

**Be specific.** Instead of "Tell me about your experience" (too broad), ask "What's a specific project you've worked on in the past 6 months? Walk me through your role, the biggest challenge, and the outcome with specific numbers."

Specificity helps candidates give you concrete examples rather than general statements.

**Include something that reveals personality.** After role-specific questions, add something that shows who they are: "Tell me about something you're learning right now and why it interests you." This helps you get a sense of the person beyond their professional qualifications.

**Step 4: Set clear expectations**

Communication makes the difference between good and poor completion rates.

**In your job posting:** "Our interview process includes an async video screening where you record responses on your schedule, followed by live interviews with our team. This helps us evaluate everyone fairly while respecting the demands on your time."

**In your application confirmation:** "Thanks for applying! If your background matches what we're looking for, you'll receive an invitation to complete a brief async video interview within 3 business days. This gives you a chance to showcase your experience beyond what fits on a resume."

**In your invitation email:**

What to expect: 4 questions, 1-2 minutes each, about 15-20 minutes total

Why this format: "Async interviews help us evaluate all candidates fairly using consistent criteria, while giving you the flexibility to record when it works best for you. You choose the timing and environment."

What happens next: We'll review responses within 3 business days and invite top candidates for live interviews

Support: Technical help link, recruiter contact, 24/7 candidate support

Details: Retakes allowed (1-2 per question), 7-day deadline, guaranteed response timeline

Clear communication helps candidates know what to expect and why you're using this approach.

**Step 5: Monitor and support the process**

During interviews, candidates record independently, but you're actively ensuring everything runs smoothly.

**Automated support:**

Send helpful reminders at 3 days and 1 day before deadline. Monitor support inbox for technical questions. Send immediate confirmation when submissions come in.

**Active monitoring:**

Track completion rates. If you sent 50 invitations and only 15 have submitted after 4 days, investigate. Normal completion rates for well-designed processes run 60-80%.

Review early submissions to catch any issues. Are questions clear? Are time limits appropriate? Address problems quickly before more candidates encounter them.

**Step 6: Review efficiently and fairly**

How you review determines whether you identify the right candidates.

**Focused review sessions.** Block 90-minute sessions to review 10-15 candidates consecutively. This creates consistency in your assessments.

**Use clear criteria.** Before watching responses, know what you're evaluating. Have specific criteria for strong, acceptable, and weak answers to each question.

**Look for substance.** A candidate who provides specific examples with measurable outcomes tells you more than someone who speaks smoothly but vaguely. You're evaluating capability, not presentation polish.

**Watch for authenticity.** Candidates who share genuine stories and speak naturally tend to give you more reliable information than those who sound overly rehearsed. Tools like Real Talk help identify overly scripted responses.

**Collaborate with your team.** Share promising candidates within the platform. Use commenting features to discuss and make collaborative decisions efficiently.

**Respond promptly.** If you promised 3-day review, honor it. Timely responses respect candidate effort and keep them engaged.

**Step 7: Connect async to your live interview process**

Async interviews are most effective when they feed into your live interview pipeline.

**Clear advancement process:**

After reviewing async responses, identify your top 15-20%. These candidates have demonstrated relevant skills and strong communication. Invite them to live interviews where you can go deeper, assess fit, and answer their questions.

**Reference their responses:** "Hi Sarah, I enjoyed your async interview, especially your example about the knowledge base project that reduced ticket time by 50%. I'd like to discuss this more and answer any questions you have about the role. Are you available for a 45-minute call this week?"

This shows you paid attention to their responses and helps create continuity between screening stages.

Your first async process will have room for improvement. Companies with high satisfaction rates continuously refine based on feedback.

**Track key metrics:**

Completion rate (target 70-85%), time to complete, where candidates drop off, candidate feedback scores, time-to-review (target under 3 business days), quality of candidates advancing.

If questions aren't giving you useful information: Make them more specific and scenario-based. Generic questions tend to produce generic answers.

**What Mistakes Decreases Your Candidate Completion Rates?**

These implementation issues separate effective async programs from ones that create friction.

**1. No personal connection anywhere**

Candidates receive an automated email, record their responses, wait, then get a form rejection. They never interact with anyone from your company.

This creates a transactional feel that doesn't reflect well on your employer brand.

Add personal elements: Have your hiring manager record a 60-second introduction video for the invitation email. After submission, send a thank-you that references something specific from their responses. When advancing candidates, send a personal note: "Hi Sarah, I really appreciated your example about the knowledge base project. Let's talk more about it."

Small touches make candidates feel valued and improve your employer brand.

**2. Too many questions**

Asking 10-12 questions that require 2-3 minutes each creates a 45-minute process. That's too long for initial screening.

You need baseline information to identify qualified candidates. That takes 3-5 well-designed questions, maximum. Keep total time to 15-20 minutes.

One company reduced questions from 8 to 4 and saw completion rates jump from 58% to 84%.

**3. Unclear time expectations**

When you ask complex questions but only allow 45 seconds, or don't allow any retakes, you create unnecessary pressure.

Match time limits to question complexity. Quick verification questions get 30-60 seconds. Behavioral questions with specific examples get 1-2 minutes. Complex scenarios get 2-3 minutes.

Allow retakes—at least one per question. This reduces pressure and often leads to better, more natural responses.

**4. Unclear purpose**

If you don't explain why you use async interviews, candidates might assume it's just to save you time without benefiting them.

Frame it clearly: "Async interviews help us evaluate all candidates using consistent criteria, while giving you the flexibility to record when it works best for you. This approach helps us make fair assessments and lets you showcase your skills beyond what fits on a resume."

Clear communication about benefits helps candidates understand the value.

**5. Technical friction or quality issues**

When platforms require apps, downloads, or complex setup, candidates abandon the process. When they encounter browser crashes or upload failures, frustration sets in.

Look for platforms that work across 99.9% of devices without requiring downloads. Willo works directly in browsers—candidates just click and start.

Also consider quality assurance. Platforms with features like Real Talk help ensure you're evaluating authentic responses, not overly rehearsed performances, which gives you more reliable assessment data.

Include a "Test Your Tech" link in invitations so candidates can verify their setup works. Provide 24/7 support so technical issues don't prevent qualified candidates from completing interviews.
