## What is a developer resume?

A developer resume is a document that presents your qualifications, work history and professional experience as a developer. While you may send a physical copy, recruiters typically request a digital version of the document. Your resume is often the first document potential employers review before determining whether you're the best candidate for the position. An effective resume can improve the likelihood of you receiving an invitation to interview and getting the role you want.Related: Software Developer vs. Web Developer (Plus IT Skills)Related jobs on Indeed

## How to write a developer resume

Follow these steps to write a resume for a developer position:### 1. Choose a resume format

Start by deciding how you want to organize sections on your resume. Below are two resume formats to choose from:- Functional: A functional resume emphasizes your skills more than work experience. Consider creating a functional resume if you're a recent graduate or if you're switching careers.
- Chronological: A chronological resume focuses more on your experience. You can use this resume format if you're looking for entry-level positions as a developer.

### 2. Write a header

Next, list your full name, e-mail address, phone number, city, and province or territory. You can also include a link to the projects you developed and your personal website. Having your contact information on your resume helps potential employers reach you to discuss the next stage in the hiring process.### 3. Include a resume objective

A resume objective is a summary of your qualifications and work history. You want this section to be engaging to encourage recruiters and hiring managers to continue reading your resume. This section provides an opportunity to briefly mention your career goals and interests.For example, you can describe whether you develop computer applications or websites. You also want to explain whether you're a front-end, back-end or full-stack developer in this section.Read more: What to Put in a Resume### 4. Describe your work experience and skill set

Use the work experience section to list developer positions you held, the company name and your employment duration. Beneath this information, explain the duties you performed in these roles and the major projects you completed.For example, you might say, "Collaborated with UI/UX designers to enhance user experience, resulting in a 30% increase in customer satisfaction scores." Including measurable achievements like this demonstrates your impact and teamwork abilities.Include any internships or co-ops you completed, as they demonstrate practical skills and familiarity with real-world projects.Next, include your soft and hard skills. While hard skills are the technical skills you learn through education and training, soft skills are abilities that relate to how you interact with others. Here are some hard and soft skills you can include in a resume for developers:- attention to detail
- application programming interface (API)
- cloud computing
- communication skills
- creativity
- database management
- data structures and algorithms
- encryption
- multitasking
- operating systems
- problem-solving skills
- product enhancement
- research
- version control

### 5. Outline your education

While you can get a developer job without a degree, employers may include postsecondary education as a requirement.In the education section, begin by listing your most advanced degree or diploma. Include the name of the school or institution, the degree or diploma earned, and the year you graduated or received the credential.You can also explain other relevant activities you participated in. For example, you can mention if you were a part of the Engineering Society or the Coding Club at your school.Related: How to Put Freelance Work on a Resume as a Web Developer### 6. List developer certifications

Certifications help employers understand your abilities as a developer. If you have obtained any, create a section on your resume and list them. You want to include relevant certifications you earned from reputable sources, such as software corporations, to validate your expertise.### 7. Highlight any supporting information

End with any information that can enhance your application. For example, you can mention hobbies and interests that relate to the role.You may also outline side projects you developed and volunteer work you completed for a developer community. Personal projects showcase your initiative, technical skills, passion for development, and commitment to continuous learning.Related: How to Write About Interests on Your Resume## Tips for creating a developer resume

Below are some tips to write a resume for developer roles:### Review the job description

Ensure you read the job description before you write your resume. Doing this can guide you on what to emphasize. For example, if the role involves creating web applications, focus on discussing your experience as an application developer.Many employers use an applicant tracking system (ATS) to screen multiple resumes, so it's essential to use keywords from the job description that apply to the role. Here are some keywords to consider including in a resume for a developer:- software development (Agile)
- code optimization
- computer science
- distributed computing
- information retrieval
- knowledge of coding languages
- programming
- software development

### Format your resume

A resume's format refers to its structure and look. You want it to be professional and readable to improve your chances of progressing to the next hiring stage.Choose professional-looking fonts, such as Arial, Calibri, Georgia, or Times New Roman. Consider a 10- or 12-point font size for section contents and a 16- to 18-point size for headers. You can also use italics or bold text for words you want to emphasize, but do not overuse these.Related: E-Mail for Developers Looking for Work (With Templates)### Keep it simple

Try to limit your resume to one page, or two at the most. This can make it easier for hiring managers and recruiters to review. You also want your resume to accurately describe how you can contribute to the company. Organize your sections to make it easy for employers to find your strengths as a developer.Related: How Long Should a Resume Be? (With Tips)### Demonstrate your value to the company

When writing your resume, it's pivotal to emphasize how you can add value as a developer. Use numbers, such as rates and percentages, to describe how you can contribute to the company using your skills and previous experience. Here are examples of how you can connect your expertise and skills to the value you can provide:- Wrote proprietary software for over 20 clients and monitored code for bugs
- Revised web security application to reduce attempted hacks by 35%
- Reworked mobile applications to improve new downloads by 78%
- Debugged legacy code to resolve system crashes, resulting in a 20% decrease in support tickets over three months
- Hosted and documented personal and client projects on GitHub, demonstrating consistent code quality and collaboration workflows

### Proofread and edit your resume

Ensure you review your resume for spelling and grammatical errors. You can create a draft and correct any mistakes you find when editing it. Consider sending the final draft to a trusted friend, career coach, or mentor to help you review it.
