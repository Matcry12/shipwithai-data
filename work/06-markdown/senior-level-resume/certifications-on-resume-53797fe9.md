## What are certifications?

Certifications are one of the resume basics: official proof of your skills and knowledge in a specific field.

These credentials come from professional organizations or schools and indicate that you've met industry standards. Adding certifications to your resume can help you impress employers. They show you're serious about your career and have the expertise needed for the job.

Certifications also give employers confidence in your abilities and can boost your chances of getting hired. Simply put, certifications can make your resume more open to new job opportunities.

## Why are certifications important?

As noted earlier, getting a certification shows you're serious about your field and backs up your skills and experience. In essence, having a professional certification on your resume is clear evidence of your skills in a specific area. For certain roles, certifications might be a requirement for consideration.

For other positions, they add extra value that can help you shine among other qualified candidates. Here are a few good reasons why it's important to have them:

**Commitment to career growth:**They demonstrate your dedication to continuous learning and professional development.**Boost confidence:**Knowing you're officially recognized for your skills can increase your confidence.**Stay updated:**Certifications keep you up-to-date with the latest knowledge and best practices in your field.**Personal satisfaction:**Earning certifications can provide a sense of personal achievement and fulfillment.**Enhance your resume:**Certifications significantly improve your resume by showcasing your qualifications and commitment.**Catch employers' eyes:**They make your application stand out, increasing your chances of being noticed by hiring managers.**Show your worth:**Certifications demonstrate that you possess the necessary skills and expertise, proving you are a valuable asset to potential employers.

## When to include certifications on your resume?

Including certifications on your resume can enhance your job prospects, but knowing when to feature them is crucial for maximizing their impact. Here are some situations where you should definitely include certifications on your resume:

### Relevant to the job

If the certification is directly relevant to the job you're applying for, it should be prominently featured on your resume. For example, if you're applying for a project management position and you have a PMP (Project Management Professional) certification, this should be highlighted in both your summary and work experience sections.

### Required by the employer

When a job listing specifies that certain certifications are required or preferred, it's essential to include these credentials on your resume. Include only certifications that you have. Never lie on your resume. This shows the employer that you meet their criteria and can increase your chances of getting an interview.

### Industry-standard

In some industries, certifications are a standard part of career progression. For instance, in IT, certifications like CompTIA A+, Cisco's CCNA, or Microsoft's MCSE are often expected. Including these certifications demonstrates that you're keeping up with industry standards.

### Enhancing qualifications

Certifications can be a great way to elevate your qualifications, especially if you lack experience in a particular area. They show you've dedicated time to gaining the knowledge and skills necessary to excel in your field.

### Career change

If you're switching careers, certifications can help ease the transition between your previous experience and your new career path. They can prove to potential employers that you have acquired relevant skills and are serious about your new career direction. For instance, if you're moving from marketing to data analysis, getting a Google Data Analytics Certificate shows employers you have the necessary skills and are serious about your new career.

### Showcasing continuous learning

Employers value candidates who show a commitment to continuous learning and professional development. Including recent certifications on your resume can illustrate your dedication to staying updated with new skills and knowledge.

### Filling employment gaps

If you have gaps in your employment history, listing certifications earned during those periods can help explain how you used that time productively. It shows that you were proactive in improving your skills and staying engaged in your field.

### Boosting credibility

Certifications can add credibility to your resume, especially from well-known and respected organizations. They can serve as third-party validation of your capabilities.

### Underlining specific talents

Certifications are an excellent way to underscore specific talents that are relevant to the job. For example, if the job requires expertise in graphic design, featuring a certification in Adobe Creative Suite can highlight your creative talents and technical skills.

A certification is an investment in your future success.

Warren Buffett

## How to list certifications on a resume?

Adding certifications to your resume is a smart way to show employers you have the expertise and dedication they're looking for. Certifications are proof that you've taken the time to learn and master specific skills. Below, you'll find the best way to list them:

**Start with the certification title:**State the full name of the certification so it's easily recognized by employers. For example, write Project Management Professional (PMP) to ensure clarity.**Mention the issuing organization:**Provide the name of the institution that awarded the certification. This adds credibility and allows employers to verify the certification if needed. For instance, Issued by Project Management Institute.**State the date you obtained it:**Include the month and year when you earned the certification. This helps employers understand how current your knowledge and skills are. For example, Obtained in January 2021.**Include the renewal date if applicable:**If the certification has an expiration or renewal date, be sure to list it. This shows that you maintain up-to-date credentials. For example, Valid until January 2026.**Add any relevant details:**Provide additional information such as a credential ID or license number, especially for certifications that require verification. Mention any special achievements or honors related to the certification. For example, License No: 12345678, Achieved with Honors.

### Should you include certifications in progress?

Don't hesitate to include certifications in progress on your resume! It shows employers that you're committed to growing your skills and staying up-to-date in your field. Just be sure to clearly mark them as "in progress" and include the expected completion date. This way, employers know you're actively working towards enhancing your qualifications. For example, you might list it as "Certified Data Analyst (In Progress, Expected Completion: August 2023)" to showcase your ongoing efforts and dedication.

### Should you list expired certifications on a resume?

Generally, you shouldn't list expired certifications on your resume. They don't hold the same value as active ones and can be confusing. However, if the expired certification is very relevant to the job, you can include it but clearly state it's expired. If you're renewing it, mention that as well. Focus on current, valid certifications and skills to make the best impression.

## Common mistakes when listing a certificate on a resume

Listing a certificate on your resume is a great way to showcase your qualifications, but it's important to do it correctly to make the right impression.

Here are some common mistakes to avoid:

**Incomplete information about certifications:**Failing to provide comprehensive details about the certification can be detrimental. Always include the full name of the certification, the issuing organization, the date of completion, and, if applicable, the expiration date. Avoid using acronyms unless they are universally recognized, as this can cause confusion and make your certifications less impactful.**Irrelevant certifications:**Including certifications that are not pertinent to the job you are applying for can clutter your resume and distract from your key qualifications. Only list certifications that directly relate to the skills and experience required for the position to ensure your resume remains focused and relevant.**Misspelling or errors:**Typos and errors in the certification name or the issuing organization's name can make you appear careless and unprofessional. Double-check the spelling and formatting to ensure everything is accurate and properly presented.**Not following formatting guidelines:**Inconsistent formatting can make your resume look unprofessional and hard to read. Ensure that the formatting for your certification section matches the rest of your resume, with clear headings, bullet points, and consistent font and text size.

## Faqs

### What's the difference between the certifications and credentials?

**Certifications**are specific recognitions awarded after completing a course or passing an exam that validates your expertise in a particular area. They are often issued by professional organizations or educational institutions and may require renewal to stay current.**Credentials**is a broader term that includes certifications, degrees, licenses, and other professional qualifications that validate a person's expertise, education, and skills. Essentially, certifications are a type of credential.

### How many certificates do you put on a resume?

The number of certificates you list on your resume depends on their relevance to the job you are applying for. Ideally, you should include 3 - 5 certifications that are most relevant to the position. Prioritize certifications that demonstrate your key skills and qualifications for the job to keep your resume focused and concise.

### Are certificates more important than degrees?

The importance of certificates versus degrees depends on the job and industry. In some fields, degrees are essential as they provide a comprehensive educational background and theoretical knowledge. However, certificates are often highly valued in industries that require specific skills and practical knowledge, as they show up-to-date expertise and specialized training. Both can be important and sometimes, having both a degree and relevant certifications can make you a more competitive candidate.

### How do I handle multiple certifications from the same organization?

If you have multiple certifications from the same organization, list them separately under the "Certifications" section. You can streamline the information by grouping them, for example: "Certified by Microsoft: Azure Solutions Architect Expert, Microsoft Certified: Data Analyst Associate."

### Can I include online certifications and courses?

Absolutely. Online certifications and courses from reputable institutions can be included, especially if they are relevant to the job. Make sure to list the name of the course, the issuing organization, and the date of completion.
