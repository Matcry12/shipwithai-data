## 5 types of engineering managers
There are five types of engineer managers you may pursue, which are:
### Tech lead
This engineering manager guides the engineering team and the product's technical direction. Individuals in this role can write code and focus on architectural direction and technical debt.
### Team lead
Managers in this role may focus more on overseeing the work environment and encouraging team members' professional development. They can also manage stakeholders and project risks.
### Delivery
Individuals in the delivery engineering manager role can prepare status reports and attend various meetings. Additionally, they focus on collaborating with stakeholders and preparing delivery roadmaps.
### Product
These individuals brainstorm, organize and prepare for upcoming projects. They spend significant time with stakeholders and end-users more than their engineering team to ensure their projects meet the client's needs and goals.
### Lead of leads
This engineering role is for large companies that require engineering managers to have their own managers. Having a lead of leads can increase productivity and quality work from teams.Please note that none of the companies mentioned in this article are affiliated with Indeed.

## How to write an engineering manager resume
Here are a few steps to help you prepare your resume for an engineer manager position:
### 1. Choose a resume format
The first step in writing an effective resume is determining which format will best organize and present your information. Many resume formats can help you emphasize various areas of your experience, skills or education, and choosing the right one for you can direct the hiring manager to the most important or impressive information.These are three resume formats you may choose:
  * Functional: A [functional resume](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/functional-resume-tips-and-examples) showcases your relevant skills and abilities. This format works best for those with robust qualifications but a short or inconsistent work experience section.
  * Chronological: This resume format is helpful if you have a lengthy and stable work experience section. It lists your experience in reverse chronological order, allowing you to highlight how your experience and previous responsibilities.
  * Combination: A resume with a combination structure can focus on work experience and skills but leaves minimal space for additional information like volunteerism or education details. It's a valuable option if you're making a career change or have disparate work experience.

Read more: [Top Resume Formats: Tips and Examples of 3 Common Resumes](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/resume-format-guide-with-examples)
### 2. List your contact information
At the top of your resume, list your contact information. Include your full name, phone number, email address and location. Your location includes the city and state where you reside. This section is vital because the hiring team can use it to contact you for an interview. If you have professional social media accounts, blogs or websites, you can also include a link here.Related: [How To Write Contact Information That Gets Your Resume Noticed](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/how-to-add-contact-information-to-your-resume)
### 3. Craft a concise professional summary
A [professional summary](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/resume-summary-examples) is a brief section that tells the hiring team who you are and what kind of experience you've had in your career. In two to three sentences, it can highlight why you might be the right candidate for the position. A professional summary's main goal is to attract a hiring manager's attention and encourage them to read the rest of your resume.To write a concise summary statement, discuss details like how many years you have in the industry, what types of engineers you have experience managing or which of your skills are strongest. You can also include elements such as achievements you earned or how you seek to help the company if they hire you.Read more: [How To Write an Effective Resume Summary (With Examples)](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/writing-a-resume-summary-with-examples)
### 4. Detail your work experience
As engineer managers can have many specialized job duties, your work experience section can be especially important. This allows you to show your previous positions and the responsibilities you completed. A hiring manager may use this information to determine your suitability for the role.For each entry, include your job title, the duration of your employment, your employer's name and location and a list of duties you performed while in that role. It's helpful to use concise and powerful language when describing your responsibilities. If you're currently employed, use present tense verbs to describe your responsibilities and incorporate past tense verbs for previous positions.Related: [How To Highlight Work Experience on Your Resume](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/write-work-experience-on-a-resume)
### 5. Include your engineer manager skills
While your work experience section allows you to show the responsibilities you manage, your skills section is an opportunity to showcase your unique or relevant abilities. This can include hard or technical skills, like familiarity with equipment or understanding of a coding language. You can also add soft capabilities like collaboration and leadership skills. Additionally, review the job description to include matching skill keywords, which can help your resume capture a recruiter's or company's attention and pass through Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) that scan resumes automatically for specific keywords.Here are other hard skills you may include on your resume as an engineer manager:
  * Agile
  * DevOps and continuous integration and continuous delivery (CI/CD)
  * Design patterns
  * Encryption
  * Distributed systems
  * Software development
  * Programmable language controller (PCL)
  * Vendor management

Related: [12 Essential Engineering Skills for Your Resume](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/essential-engineering-skills)
### 6. Highlight your education and certifications
It's beneficial to create a new section to highlight your educational background. Include the type of degree you have and the institute that issued it. As an engineer manager, you may have an advanced degree to show organizations you can lead other engineers and fulfill your job responsibilities effectively. Some degrees engineering managers obtain include:
  * Bachelor's degree in engineering
  * Bachelor's degree in architecture
  * Master's degree in engineering management

After your education, if you have or are currently obtaining a certification, create a new section to highlight it. Include the certification title, the host organization's name and the year you received it or the credentials expire. If you're currently in the process of obtaining a certification or other degree, you may list this along with the expected graduation date. This supplemental knowledge may show your career dedication and differentiate you from other candidates. Related: [10 Engineering Degree Options To Consider for Your Career](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/career-development/engineering-degree-options)
### 7. Thoroughly edit and proofread
Before submitting your resume, edit and proofread it. This allows you to find improvement areas, including fixing grammar or spelling mistakes. Presenting a polished resume that's free of errors can communicate to the hiring team you're detail-oriented and passionate about quality.You can ask friends, relatives or colleagues to review your resume or use an online resume-editing program. There are also grammar-checking programs that can help you improve your clarity and avoid grammar and spelling mistakes.Read more: [27 Proofreading Tips That Will Improve Your Resume](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/proofreading-tips-that-will-improve-your-resume)
## Why is writing an effective resume important?
[Resumes are important](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/why-is-a-resume-important) because they're essential for showing your qualifications for a job within your application. These are some benefits of an effective resume:
### Capture the reader's attention
Before your resume can showcase your value, it gains the reader's attention enough to encourage them to continue reviewing it. A simple but impressive aesthetic and concise details can make an effective resume distinctive to a hiring specialist.
### Emphasize your skills and experience
Hiring managers seek qualified candidates and often look to provide their organization with someone ready to assume the job's responsibilities without substantial education or training. By highlighting your experience and skills, your resume can emphasize to a company that you're the right individual.
### Communicate your unique value
Depending on the position, the hiring team may consider many candidates besides you. This makes your resume critical because it's a tool you can use to display how you're unique.
### Encourage hiring managers to schedule interviews
A resume aims to represent you well enough that the hiring team contacts you to plan an interview. You know it's effective when your document advances you in the hiring process.
