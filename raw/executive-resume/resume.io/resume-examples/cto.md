## What does a CTO do?
The CTO, or chief technology officer, is the guiding force behind a company’s information technology strategy and management. Like all other top executives, the CTO must be a strategic thinker as well as an excellent communicator. A key aspect of the job is keeping up-to-date on all technological advances and making decisions about the company’s IT roadmap.
Alongside IT knowledge, CTOs must be process-oriented so that they can develop and implement policies that further the company’s goals while ensuring the consistent and smooth operation of technology.
CTOs are also charged with overseeing the safety of IT systems, monitoring budgets, and analyzing data to look for enhancements to efficiency.
### CTO job market and outlook
Because you are at the top of the technology job market, you may need to be flexible about your location when you enter the job search. Finding the right CTO position will be easier in a major tech hub such as Silicon Valley, Calif.; Austin, Texas; Seattle or New York.
According to the [alphalist CTO podcast](https://alphalist.com/blog/landing-your-ideal-cto-role-in-2023-navigating-the-job-market-and-finding-the-right-fit), the tightening of funding for startups has corresponded to a tightening in the job market as well. That does not mean there are no jobs out there for you; it just means you should be careful in evaluating your opportunities.
CTOs with seven or more years of experience can expect to earn a base salary of more than $222,000 plus additional compensation of almost $56,000, [BuiltIn](https://builtin.com/salaries/dev-engineer/cto-chief-technology-officer) reports. For those new to the position, salaries will run about $140,000. If a big paycheck is your priority, try Washington, D.C., or Miami.

## How to write a CTO resume
When it comes to your CTO resume, predictability is a good thing. Recruiters don’t want surprises in the sections your choose (or their names), they want to be surprised by your excellent professional achievements. 
Therefore, you should stick to the following common elements:
  * The [resume header](https://resume.io/blog/resume-header)
  * The [resume summary](https://resume.io/blog/resume-summary) (aka profile or personal statement)
  * The [employment history section](https://resume.io/blog/writing-work-experience-on-a-resume)
  * The [resume skills section](https://resume.io/blog/list-skills-on-your-resume)
  * The [education section](https://resume.io/blog/list-education-on-your-resume)


## Choosing the best resume format for a CTO
As a technology expert, you may be tempted to [choose a format](https://resume.io/blog/resume-formats) that allows you to list all your IT knowledge, but don’t give in to that. We recommend the standard reverse [chronological order structure](https://resume.io/blog/chronological-resume-format), which will give you the freedom to illustrate your strategic and management successes as well as your technical understanding.
If, however, the job for which you are applying requires many advanced technology skills, you may opt for a [hybrid style resume](https://resume.io/blog/resume-hybrid-format), because it allows for the expansion of your skills section without sacrificing space within your employment history.
### Resume header
The main consideration of your CTO resume header is to attract attention to your [contact information](https://resume.io/blog/resume-contact-information) and social media accounts. Do this by using [legible fonts](https://resume.io/blog/what-is-the-best-font-to-use-in-a-resume) with your name in larger or bold type. Also include your email, phone and city of residence. Your street address is optional.
The secondary purpose of the header is to leave a positive and memorable first visual impression.
If you have a technology portfolio, list that here, too. A portfolio (which you can link to directly from your resume when you use our builder tool) affords you the space to expand on IT projects you can only mention in your resume.
## Resume summary example 
The summary of your CTO resume is your best opportunity to distinguish yourself by personalizing a message that meshes with the company culture. Yes, you should summarize your career, but you should also choose your most [impressive achievement](https://resume.io/blog/how-to-write-accomplishments-for-your-resume): making sure it illustrates an attribute the company seeks.
Because fit is so important in your role, add a sentence that makes it clear why you are the right person for this job. 
Adaptable summary resume sample 
Experienced and forward-thinking Chief Technology Officer (CTO) with 20+ years of extensive experience leading cross-functional teams and driving technological advancement in various industries.

## Employment history sample
You’ve already had a long and distinguished career, so the biggest challenge for your CTO resume employment history will be choosing which accomplishments to highlight. Don’t go back any [further than 10 years](https://resume.io/blog/how-far-should-a-resume-go-back) (if that) since recruiters want to know what you have done at higher levels.
You don’t have a lot of space here, so choose successes that pack the biggest punch and are directly related to the role you aspire to. Make sure you explain your role, your actions, and your results using data and details to strengthen your claims.

  2. Anticipate technology trends
  3. [Strong communication](https://resume.io/blog/communication-skills)
  4. Flexibility
  5. Discipline
  6. Pragmatism

## Resume layout and design
A chief technology officer’s role is a serious and thoughtful one and the layout of your CTO resume should reflect that. Aim for simplicity and clarity over artiness. Definitely save the fancy charts and graphs for a work demonstration.
Here are a few pointers for developing your layout:
  * Choose opposing fonts for body text and headings. For instance, use a san serif for the text and a serif type for the header.
  * Make sure your name and headers draw in the eye.
  * Although your career has been long and illustrious, don’t try to cram too much onto the page. The font size should be at least 11 points and the [margins](https://resume.io/blog/resume-margins) at least one-half inch (although 1 inch is better).
  * If you don’t want to take your time designing and formatting your resume, use one of our HR-vetted and [expertly-designed resume templates](https://resume.io/resume-templates).
