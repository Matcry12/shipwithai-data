## What to include in a director of engineering resume
Your director of engineering resume should be a reflection of your expertise, [professionalism](https://au.indeed.com/career-advice/career-development/the-ultimate-guide-to-professionalism) and value. Here are the main elements to include.
### Contact information
Your name, location, phone number and professional email address represent the essential contact information that every recruiter looks for at first glance, so you can be easily contacted.
### Summary statement
Your summary statement needs to showcase you as an ideal candidate. It needs to present your qualifications, skills and achievements in a way that is intriguing and engaging.
### Education and certifications
This section highlights your academic background and any professional certifications relevant to the position. They serve as evidence of your knowledge and preparedness for the role.
### Relevant experience
This section needs to include detailed descriptions of our past roles and accomplishments. Describe specifically how you contributed to your previous teams and organisations. You can use bullet points to keep it concise.
### Key skills
List the technical and soft skills that you have, but ensure these are relevant to the position you are applying for. For a director role, leadership and management skills may be especially sought-after.Related: [How to Create an Effective Resume Layout (With Template)](https://au.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/resume-layout)
## Tips for writing a director of engineering resume
Here are three valuable tips for writing your resume.
### Highlight achievements
Rather than just listing your job roles, focus on your achievements. Use active verbs and depict the results of your actions in percentage or quantifiable terms.
### Use keywords
Align your resume with the job description. You can do this by using the keywords listed in the job posting within your resume. This can increase the chance of your resume being noticed by your prospective employer.
### Tailor your approach
Every company is unique, and so is their culture and needs. Avoid sending identical resumes to each potential employer. Tailor your resume to best suit the company and role you are applying for.Read more: [10 Best Skills to Include on a Resume](https://au.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/best-resume-skills)
## Frequently asked questions
The following are some frequently asked questions about director of engineering resumes.
### What should the ideal length of a director of engineering resume be?
Although there's no one-size-fits-all rule when it comes to resume length, a director of engineering resume should typically be two pages long.At this level, you have a wealth of relevant experience and skills to show, and this often requires a bit more space. However, remember, every word in your resume should add value and be necessary, so avoid unnecessary filler phrases.
### Can voluntary experience be added to the resume?
Absolutely! Volunteering or unpaid internships work particularly well if they're relevant to the job you're applying for or if you're a new graduate with less professional experience. It highlights your commitment and passion for your work, which can make you stand out as a candidate.
### Should references be included in the resume?
It's no longer necessary to include references in your resume unless specifically asked for in the job advertisement. Employers typically assume they can ask for references if needed. Instead, use that extra space to emphasise your skills and achievements.
### Do I need to include a cover letter with my resume?
While not always requested in job advertisements, a cover letter can set you apart from other candidates. It's an opportunity to summarise your skills and experiences and explain why you are uniquely qualified for the position. In essence, it's another tool you can use and which should be tailored to each job application.
### How do I make my resume stand out?
Making your resume stand out among many applications is key. Some ways to achieve this include quantifying your achievements, using active verbs, demonstrating a clear progression in your career and tailoring your resume to the specific job requirements. You may also want to consider a clean, professional design that makes your resume more readable.Related:
