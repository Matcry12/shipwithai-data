**Looking for a new job after a lengthy career break and not sure where to start?**
Whether your career break was due to travel, [redundancy](https://www.reed.co.uk/career-advice/how-to-deal-with-redundancy/), further study, personal illness, family commitments, or something else – knowing how to address it on your CV can be a challenge. But the biggest mistake you can make is to ignore it.
The example we’ve used in this template is for a break brought on by family commitments, but for more specific help, you can read our guide on [how to explain a gap in your CV](https://www.reed.co.uk/career-advice/how-to-explain-a-gap-in-your-cv/).

## **What is a career break?**
A career break is an extended period away from your usual employment, typically lasting several months or years. Career break reasons can include:
  * **Family commitments** – raising children, caring for elderly relatives
  * **Health and wellbeing** – recovery from illness, mental health support
  * **Education and development** – further study, [professional qualifications](https://www.reed.co.uk/courses/professional)
  * **Personal growth** – travel, volunteering, pursuing passions
  * **Life transitions** – relocation, redundancy, [career changes](https://www.reed.co.uk/career-advice/changing-careers-what-you-need-to-know/)


Taking a [sabbatical](https://www.reed.co.uk/career-advice/what-is-a-sabbatical/) year or extended career break is increasingly common, with many employers recognising the value of well-rounded candidates who bring diverse life experiences to their roles.

  1. **Contact details**
  2. [**Personal statement**](https://www.reed.co.uk/career-advice/how-to-write-a-personal-statement/)

  7. [**References**](https://www.reed.co.uk/career-advice/how-to-get-a-reference/)


## **How to write your CV after a career break**
Ready to turn your time away into your next big selling point? Here’s how to reshape your CV and show employers you’re more than prepared for a strong comeback.
### **Contact details**
Keep it simple and professional. Include your:
  * Full name
  * Location (city and postcode)
  * Phone number
  * Email address
  * LinkedIn profile (if you have one)


### **Personal statement**
Your [personal statement](https://www.reed.co.uk/career-advice/how-to-write-a-personal-statement/) is crucial when returning from a career break. This 3-4 sentence summary should answer three key questions:
  * **Who are you?** Briefly describe your professional background
  * **What do you offer?** Highlight your key skills and experience
  * **What are your aims?** Express your enthusiasm for returning to work


Mention your career break briefly (just one line) and focus on your eagerness to re-enter the workforce. Save detailed explanations for your cover letter.
> **Example personal statement:**
> _Experienced marketing professional with five years’ experience in digital campaigns and brand management. Recently completed a career break to focus on family commitments, during which I maintained industry knowledge through online courses and freelance projects. Now seeking to return to a marketing role where I can apply my strategic thinking and creative skills to drive business growth._
### **Skills**
This section is particularly important for career break CVs. Lead with skills that remain relevant, regardless of when you last used them professionally. Focus on:
**Hard skills:**
  * Software proficiency (Microsoft Office, Adobe Creative Suite, etc.)
  * Technical abilities
  * Industry-specific knowledge
  * Languages


**Soft skills:**
  * Communication
  * Problem-solving
  * Project management
  * Leadership


Provide specific examples where possible. If you’ve maintained or developed skills during your break through volunteering, courses, or personal projects, mention these.
### **Employment history**
List your [work experience](https://www.reed.co.uk/career-advice/how-to-get-work-experience/) in reverse chronological order. For your career break, you have two options:
**Option 1: Brief explanation of your career break**
Include a simple line such as:
> **‘Career break – family commitments (2020–2023)’ or ‘Sabbatical – travel and personal development (2021–2022)’.**
While not essential, you can choose to add more detail about any relevant skills, volunteering, personal projects, or courses you completed during this time – but only if it’s relevant to the job you’re applying for.
Adding this detail is particularly useful for people who don’t have any recent work experience to note. 
**Option 2: Focus on productive activities instead of labelling it as a career break**
If you spent your break doing freelance work, volunteering, or gaining new qualifications, list these as roles in your employment history. 
> **For example:**
> **Freelance Marketing Consultant** (2021–2023)– Provided digital marketing support to three local businesses
>   * Completed Google Analytics certification
>   * Managed social media accounts, increasing engagement by 25%Completed Google Analytics certification
> 

For employment before your break, focus on achievements rather than just duties. Use numbers and specific examples to demonstrate your impact. This means using the [STAR method](https://www.reed.co.uk/career-advice/star-method-what-you-need-to-know/) (Situation, Task, Action, Result) to describe your achievements, even from unpaid work.
### **Education**
Include your qualifications from GCSE level onwards in reverse chronological order. 
If you’ve completed any [courses](https://www.reed.co.uk/courses/) or gained certifications during your career break, include these prominently. They show you’ve used your time productively and stayed current in your field.
> **Example:**
>   * Google Analytics Certified (2022)
>   * Project Management Course, Coursera (2021)
>   * BA (Hons) Business Studies, University of Manchester (2005-2008), 2:1
> 

### **Hobbies and interests**
This optional section can be valuable for career break CVs as it helps fill potential gaps and shows you as a well-rounded person. Only include interests that:
  * Demonstrate relevant skills
  * Show personal development
  * Are genuinely interesting to discuss in interviews


Avoid generic entries like ‘socialising with friends’ – instead, opt for specific activities that showcase desirable qualities.
### **References**
Simply state ‘References available upon request’. Prepare a separate list of referees, including:
  * Former managers or colleagues
  * Academic contacts
  * Professional mentors
  * Volunteer coordinators (if applicable)


A [reference](https://www.reed.co.uk/career-advice/how-to-get-a-reference/) from a previous employer can be particularly valuable, showing you left on good terms and maintained professional relationships.
## **Top tips for career break CVs**
### **Do:**
  * **Be honest** about your career break – trying to hide it often backfires
  * **Focus on achievements** rather than just listing responsibilities
  * **Highlight transferable skills** gained during your break
  * **Keep it relevant** – tailor your CV to each job application
  * **Show enthusiasm** for returning to work
  * **Use**[**active language**](https://www.reed.co.uk/career-advice/what-words-should-i-use-on-my-cv/) and positive framing


### **Don’t:**
  * **Apologise** for your career break – it’s a legitimate life choice
  * **Leave obvious gaps** without explanation
  * **Undersell yourself** – your experience is still valuable
  * **Include irrelevant details** about your personal life
  * **Forget to proofread** – attention to detail matters


## **Career break CV examples**
The following examples show how you can address a career break within the Work Experience section of your CV. These snippets demonstrate how to explain a gap clearly, while focusing on skills gained and readiness to return to work.
### **Example 1: Family career break**
**Career Break**
_2019–2023_
Took time away from finance roles to focus on family commitments. During this period, completed AAT Level 4 qualification and maintained up-to-date knowledge of industry trends. Now ready to apply refreshed skills in a new finance position.
### **Example 2: Travel career break**
**Career Break – Travel and Volunteering**
_2022–2023_
Travelled throughout Southeast Asia, developing cultural awareness and conversational language skills. Volunteered with local charities, improving communication skills and adaptability. Keen to return to sales management with a worldwide perspective.
### **Example 3: Further study career break**
**Career Break – Further Education**
_2022_
Completed a full-time Digital Marketing Diploma, with a focus on SEO, analytics, and campaign strategy. Used this time to build new technical skills and keep knowledge current, ready to contribute fresh ideas to a marketing team.
Remember, CVs should be succinct ([no longer than two pages](https://www.reed.co.uk/career-advice/how-long-should-a-cv-be/)). If you’re looking to provide more detailed context, it’s best to do it in your [cover letter](https://www.reed.co.uk/career-advice/career-break-cover-letter-template/) and/or at the [interview](https://www.reed.co.uk/career-advice/how-to-prepare-for-an-interview/).
## **Tips for getting back into the job market after a career break**
Once your CV is polished, here are some practical steps for your job search:
  1. [**Network**](https://www.reed.co.uk/career-advice/what-is-networking/)**actively** – reconnect with former colleagues and industry contacts
  2. **Consider**[**temporary work**](https://www.reed.co.uk/career-advice/how-to-make-yourself-available-for-temporary-work/) – it can be a good way to ease back into employment
  3. **Attend industry events** – shows you’re serious about your return
  4. **Update your LinkedIn** – ensure it matches your CV and shows you’re actively job hunting
  5. [**Prepare for interviews**](https://www.reed.co.uk/career-advice/interview-techniques/preparing-for-a-job-interview/) – be ready to discuss your career break confidently and positively


## **Final thoughts**
Remember, taking a career break shows you’re someone who makes thoughtful life decisions and isn’t afraid to prioritise what matters most – and these are valuable qualities that many employers appreciate.
Your career break doesn’t define your professional worth – your skills, experience, and enthusiasm for getting back to work do. With a well-crafted CV that presents your career break positively and highlights your continued value as an employee, you’ll be well on your way to landing a job you’ll love.
The most important thing is to approach your [job search](https://www.reed.co.uk/career-advice/job-search-dos-donts/) with confidence. You’ve got this.
