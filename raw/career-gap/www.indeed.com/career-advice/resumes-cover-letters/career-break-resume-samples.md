## Why you should address a career break in your resume
If you've had a gap in employment at any point in your career, addressing it upfront is a good practice when applying for jobs.
> Be proactive about sharing any gaps in employment. The past stigma of career breaks has changed significantly. Speak to the break and convey the accomplishments attained during this time. There are many transferrable skills learned through life experiences.—[Carolynn Bruce](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/author/carolynn-bruce), SHRM-SCP
By including information about your career break in your resume, you can experience several positive benefits for your job search:
  * Employers may appreciate your unique perspective: The way people perceive gaps in employment has changed over time, and many employers understand that everyone has a different career path and timeline. Discussing your career break on your resume shows employers about your unique, non-linear career path and the benefits it can bring to their company.
  * You can avoid assumptions before they happen: Some hiring managers may have a bias against people with gaps on their resume, so it's a good idea to explain your career break to avoid any negative assumptions. Sharing your experience allows you to provide context to the employer and explain your career break from a positive perspective.
  * Discussing your career break can highlight various skills: Top candidates for positions often find ways to differentiate themselves from other candidates by emphasizing unique skills and interesting experiences. Mentioning your time away from your career can give you a platform to discuss skills and abilities that other candidates don't even think to mention.
  * The interview can focus on your skills and experience: By addressing your career break in your resume, you can avoid having to explain your employment gaps during the interview. This allows you to focus more on your qualifications and professional experience.

## How to explain a career break in your resume
If you're ready to update your resume to include a career break, follow these steps:
### 1. Be honest about your experience
An important aspect of appropriately discussing a career break in your resume is being honest about your experience. You can decide how much information you're comfortable discussing, but make sure that the information you share is accurate.
> A career break can be a differentiator to leverage. Share how your break from paid employment influenced your career goals and led you to the position you seek.—[Carolynn Bruce](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/author/carolynn-bruce), SHRM-SCP
Find a positive way to discuss your experience to be authentic and genuine with potential employers.Related: [How To Plan for a Career Break](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/career-development/career-break)
### 2. Describe your recent career break in your summary or objective
Include a resume summary or objective at the top of your application where you mention the reason for your career break and explain how taking a break set you up for success in your next position. This works best for people who are trying to find a job directly after a career break. If you've had a steady job since your career break, you may want to simply reference it in the experience section of your resume.Related: [How to Explain Gaps in Employment](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/employment-gaps-on-resume)
### 3. Remove months of employment
Only including the years when listing past positions can make your career break seem more reasonable in the context of the rest of your career. This reminds employers that your career break is just a small time period in the context of your professional history.
### 4. List your career break as a sabbatical
In the experience section of your resume, list your career break as if it were a job. Instead of a job title and company, you can list "personal sabbatical," "family leave" or other similar phrases to account for any periods when you didn't have a job. If you developed any special skills during your time off, did freelance work, or completed volunteer projects, you can list this information in bullet points under the career sabbatical section.Related: [Q&A: What Is a Sabbatical?](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/career-development/what-is-a-sabbatical)
