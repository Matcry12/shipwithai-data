### How to negotiate?

Every company has a hiring budget. Let's say a company has **₹1 Cr to hire 4 candidates**. Here's how salary negotiation plays out:

### Scenario:

* 3 candidates accept **₹20 LPA** each.* The 4th candidate negotiates smartly and secures **₹40 LPA** (*since 20+20+20+40 = 1 Cr*).* But here's the twist — the **4th candidate doesn't join**! Instead, they leverage the offer and crack a **₹60 LPA** deal elsewhere.

### **What does a smart HR do?**

* Knowing they now have **₹40 LPA** left, they increase the existing 3 candidates' salaries to **₹30 LPA** if they negotiate.* With the remaining **₹10 LPA**, they hire a junior candidate.

### 🎭 End Result:

* 3 candidates are now at ₹30 LPA.* The 4th candidate bagged a ₹60 LPA job.* The HR successfully managed the budget.* The junior candidate got hired (without knowing all this 😅)

### Now, how to quote a number to HR?

If you are in a ***service-based company*** and your salary is around 3–10 lakhs [3–5 years of experience], and you are applying in a product-based company, then you can simply quote around 2X of your current salary at least. Which is around 25–30 LPA.

If you are in a ***product-based company*** and your salary is around 15–20 lakhs [3–5 years of experience], and you are applying in a big product-based company, then you can quote 2–3X of your current, i.e, 45–50 lakhs.

At least go for a 100–150% hike, this hike you will get only after a few offers, if you get this hike in your first offer, only, that's great. **You are a champion!!**

Use **LeetCode wisely**, check the latest offers that are rolled out by that company, are similar companies just to get an idea of how the market is right now, then quote your number accordingly. Always do proper research before applying for a job.
