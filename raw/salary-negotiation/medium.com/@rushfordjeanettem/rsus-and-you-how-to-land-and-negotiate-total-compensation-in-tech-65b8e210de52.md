#### Joining Atlassian was an eye-opener for me

I'll be honest — before joining Atlassian, I had no idea how Total Compensation (TC) worked at tech companies. Over time, I've come to understand why software engineers in tech enjoy such competitive compensation packages. If you've ever wondered what makes TC so appealing, let me break it down for you and demystify the process.

### Let's Start With Salaries

The first — and most straightforward — component of TC is your annual salary. This is typically the number listed in job postings and can vary based on your location (especially for remote roles) and skill level. While some salary ranges seem unreasonably broad, they are often intended to reflect varying levels of experience within a role.

What's important to know is that this number represents your guaranteed income — your bi-weekly paycheck. It's the foundation of your TC and comparable to what you'd expect in most corporate roles.

💡 **Pro Tip:** Don't let the listed salary range stop you from applying. Always dig deeper and ask recruiters about the *total* package, as the salary is often just the starting point.

### Bonus Pay

Next up: bonuses. Many tech companies offer annual bonuses, and some even provide quarterly bonuses. Bonuses are often calculated as a percentage of your base salary, and their final amount may depend on your performance, the company's performance, or both.

While bonuses can vary, they're a nice addition to your overall compensation. Be sure to ask your recruiter for specifics on how bonuses are calculated, as this can differ greatly between companies.

### The Game-Changer: Equity

Here's where TC in tech truly stands out — **equity**. If you're unfamiliar with Reserved Stock Units (RSUs), they're essentially company stock that's granted to you as part of your compensation package. Unlike employee stock purchase plans, RSUs are provided as grants and come with a vesting schedule.

Let's break it down with an example:

* **Year 1 Grant:** $75,000 vested over 4 years = $18,750/year* **Year 2 Grant:** $100,000 vested over 4 years = $25,000/year* **Year 3 Grant:** $125,000 vested over 4 years = $31,250/year* **Year 4 Grant:** $150,000 vested over 4 years = $37,500/year

By Year 4, you'd have $112,500 in vested equity annually, in addition to your salary and bonus. Equity grants start to stack as you stay with the company, creating a significant income stream. And as you're promoted, the value of your grants typically increases, replacing older ones with larger grants.

💡 **Why Equity Matters:** Once vested, you can sell your shares at the current market value. If the stock price rises, your equity value increases, adding a wealth-building component to your compensation. Of course, stock values can fluctuate, so it's wise to diversify your portfolio or strategize based on market conditions.

### Why Total Compensation in Tech Is So Unique

Tech companies often use TC as a way to reward and retain top talent. The combination of competitive salaries, bonuses, and equity offers a more holistic package than most traditional industries. Equity, in particular, creates a unique opportunity to build long-term wealth, especially in growing companies.

For professionals looking to maximize their earning potential and long-term financial stability, understanding and leveraging TC is crucial.

Start by searching jobs on platforms like Glassdoor and Levels.fyi to determine if they include Reserved Stock Units (RSUs) as part of their compensation packages. These platforms provide detailed breakdowns, such as the pay structure for a Software Engineer at Atlassian. While this is just one example, it illustrates how RSUs can significantly contribute to total compensation.

When you hear about someone earning hundreds of thousands of dollars, it might seem unbelievable — until you consider how much of that could be in stock grants. RSUs are a valuable part of many tech compensation packages and shouldn't be overlooked when evaluating offers.

### How to Negotiate RSUs in the Offer Phase

Understanding RSUs starts with research. Platforms like Glassdoor and Levels.fyi provide insight into how many RSUs you can expect per year for a given role. However, the next step is to talk directly with the recruiter to get specifics for the position you're applying for.

If the base salary isn't as high as you were hoping for, you can often negotiate for a higher RSU grant to make up the difference. For example, if you're offered $100k per year but were aiming for $110k, you might request an additional $40k in RSUs over a four-year vesting period. This adjustment would add $10k per year to your compensation while also aligning with the company's equity structure.

Remember, RSUs often stack annually as you continue with the company, increasing your total earnings over time. By understanding how RSUs work and asking informed questions during the offer phase, you'll be better equipped to negotiate a package that reflects your worth.
