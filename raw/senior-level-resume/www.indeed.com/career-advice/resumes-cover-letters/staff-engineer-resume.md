When applying to a senior-level engineering position, employers typically expect you to submit a resume with the rest of your application. A resume helps the hiring manager determine if you have enough qualifications to be a leader in an engineering role. If you're applying to a staff engineer job, it's helpful to learn how to write an impressive resume to help you secure a job.In this article, we discuss how to write a staff engineer resume by explaining what to include in one, listing five steps to create this document and providing a resume template and example.

## What to include in a staff engineer resume
Here are some important components to include in a staff engineer resume:
  * Skills: As a staff engineer, it's important to display your skills on a resume to show the employer that you're a qualified candidate. You can make a skills section to list any relevant abilities you have on your resume.
  * Examples: Having examples on your resume helps the hiring manager learn what value you can add to the company. Try to use adjectives to describe yourself and numbers to show how you helped in your previous roles.
  * Leadership experience: Since being a staff engineer is a managerial position, it's important to show that you have some experience in leadership. You can do this when writing about your work history or when adding your skills.

## How to write a staff engineer resume
Here are five steps to write a staff engineer resume:
### 1. Add your contact information
To start your resume, add your contact information to the top of the document. Write your first and last name, and then, on the next line, add your phone number, email address and location. If you have a professional portfolio or a link to a networking site, you can add that in this section too. It's helpful to include your contact information so the hiring manager can reach you to schedule an interview.Related: [Top 30 Keywords To List on a Software Engineer Resume](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/software-engineering-resume-keywords)
### 2. Develop your professional summary
Next, develop your professional summary. A professional summary is a brief paragraph that describes yourself as an employee. It typically includes an overview of your qualifications, including your experience, skills or education. Sometimes, professional summaries state what type of job or company you're seeking. A professional summary is important because gives the employer an idea of who you are as a professional.Read more: [How To Write an Effective Engineer Manager Resume in 7 Steps](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/engineer-manager-resume)
### 3. List your work experience
After you create your professional summary, list your work experience. Since a staff engineer position is a higher role, it's essential that you include your work history on your resume. This shows that you're a qualified engineer who can be successful in a managerial position. Start by listing your most recent role, the dates you worked there and the name and location of the company. Then, create a list with three to five bullet points explaining your job duties for this role.Related: [Understanding the 10 Career Levels for Software Engineers](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/finding-a-job/engineer-level)
### 4. Include your education
Once you list your work experience, include your education. Most staff engineers have at least a bachelor's degree in engineering or a related subject. To show that you have the proper amount of education for this job, list your most recent degree. Then, add the name of the academic institution and the date that you graduated. If you have other credentials or accomplishments, you can include that here too.Related: [Engineer in Training: Duties and How To Get Certified](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/career-development/engineer-in-training)
### 5. Write about your skills
Next, write about your skills in a separate section. Staff engineers use several skills each day to complete their duties. Including your skills in your resume tells the hiring manager that you can fulfill the role. Try to list both hard and soft skills in this section to show you have a wide range of abilities and professional traits.Read more: [12 Essential Engineering Skills for Your Resume](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/essential-engineering-skills)
