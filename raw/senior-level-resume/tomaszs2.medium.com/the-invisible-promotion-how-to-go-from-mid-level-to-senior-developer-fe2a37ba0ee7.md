### Who's Really Mid-Level vs. Senior?

There's no strict line between mid-level and senior developer, it's not an official term. However, usually when people talk about these skill levels they have some expectations and there's some level of agreement on what they represent, but it's rather a scale, and it changed since the emerge of AI and role [inflation](https://tomaszs2.medium.com/junior-developer-title-disappeared-before-the-rise-of-ai-a25ec029b818). I'll describe the **real** role descriptions here, not the inflated ones.

In the technical path we can distinguish four roles: junior, mid, senior and principal engineer. Junior is someone who just started work, or works for several years, mid developer is someone who worked for several years already, senior has a longer tenure, while principal engineer already worked for a long time. I'd say junior is between 0–4 years, mid: 5–9 years, senior: 10–15 years and principal: 15+ years of experience. One thing to notice here is that everyone assumes other periods of time, and there are cases where such ranges don't apply, and it's because formerly mentioned role inflation.

From the task handling perspective **junior developers** need a lot of help and guidance on every task, down to basic things like programming languages, frameworks operations. It doesn't necessarily mean they need to ask for people help, because now we have AI too, but they have to research and learn on almost every step of the work. If it comes to AI usage, they use it, but the generated code is usually unacceptable, because they can't due to lack of knowledge and experience know if the generated code is proper or not. So they need also heavy guidance towards using AI-assistive techniques more of a learning booster than replacer.

**Mid-level developer** already gained some experience and is comfortable handling most of the day-to-day tasks. He knows how to get information and is able to write code that does what it supposed to do. Usually it's aligned with the needs. If it comes to AI usage, mid-level developer can use AI assistive tools to generate bigger pieces of code and can check if it's aligning with the way rest of the code is written. So in the mid-level role person reaches the level where it can adhere to standards and rules and knows how to do standard tasks. Still needs help sometimes.

**Senior-developer** starts where mid-level developer goes beyond what's standard. She knows how to do majority of the tasks, the code is good and knows how to gather needed information. But it's more than that, senior-developer also starts to think broader: is the solution scalable? will we able to maintain this code? do we need to change something in the implementation or way of work to improve overall quality? So it's not just about aligning and learning, it is now about going beyond the standard grind. If it comes to AI usage, senior-developer should be in a process of developing skills and methods to use it effectively to optimise his efficiency and quality of code. Lastly, senior developer can take up more complicated, complex tasks and finish them.

Another distinctive feature between these roles, in context of **principal engineer**, is collaboration and mentoring. Junior developer is only the receiver of support and mentoring, moving to mid-level role he helps junior developers, but still is receiving support.

Senior developer helps juniors, middlers, and other senior developers in the team, collaborates with them to build better software, while principal engineer goes even beyond that and uses his/her knowledge and experience to shape the development process, introduces best practices, onboards to them, and aligns whole team or teams around ways of working and collaboration giving the tone for the whole engineering effort.

### What Separates Mid-Level from Senior Engineers?

As we can see there's a difference between mid-level and senior engineer. Moving to senior role you should already be able to do majority of standard tasks, be able to move from individual, competitive mindset to collaborative one, help other team members, be able to find patterns and improve the overall maintainability of the code if it's via refactoring, standardization, layering or other best practices and he can pursue complex tasks.

### How Companies Benefit from Senior-Level Engineers

While deciding who to hire: middle developer or senior developer it's important to understand the differences I've mentioned before. From the company perspective hiring a senior developer over a middle developer is useful because senior developer will:

* support and mentor junior and middle developers* solve problems that can't be solved by junior and middle engineers* improve the project and operation* help keep up the quality of the project* collaborate with other to reach goals

It distinguishes senior-level engineers from mid-level ones. Since senior developers have experience and knowledge they use the available time and resources to grow beyond the individual contributor role thinking more on a project and operational level rather than on code level.

### The Skills That Turn You Into a Senior Engineer

Developing skills needed for a senior engineering role requires stepping outside of your comfort zone, but it's doable:

1. **Prioritize team being in the zone rather than you being in the zone**

When being middle developer you focus on doing your work good. It means also going into the space called the zone, and tuning down all outside signals. As a senior engineer, you have to stop doing that, and you need to help others, so that they can do their work too.

It means answering to messages right away, helping other developers, doing code reviews on time etc. It means **moving from the competitive, individual contributor role to a team player** that knows that he's in a team that has to succeed, not only him. Things like reporting, communication, code reviews, even JIRA stop being something outside of your job-description, you treat them as a part of your job.

Since you're reader of my articles, I don't think it applies to you, but I have to, as a side note, also tell that gate keeping and "it's a tribe knowledge" attitude is also something person has to get rid of. Mostly it happens already while people hold junior positions, but can happen with any role decoration.

**2. Become a better communicator**

Large portion of being a software engineer is about communication. You have to communicate with designers, frontend developers or backend developers, PM, lead, product owner, stakeholders, other senior engineers, but also middle and junior engineers. Effective communication means you're upfront, clear, constructive and helpful.

Instead of not bringing up things that matter, you start to initiate the difficult conversations that have to be initiated so that the things can move further.

**3. Move out from the god syndrome**

It's widely documented at at some point into any craft we move from knowing nothing to the god syndrome. We know everything, everything is suddenly clear and we know the best answer to every problem and question. Is it being a principal engineer? Not really, it happens somewhere around exactly being a middle developer.

The thing is that it's blocking your growth. It closes you from other perspectives, approaches, and can make you a pain in the ass for other people to work with you. If you reached the god syndrome, enjoy it, but not be overconfident, start to grow this attitude where you can actually take into account other people have way more experience than you do, and maybe you don't take into account factors that's beyond your current expertise level. Be curious, ask, learn.

**4. Don't exaggerate**

Everything that's too early and too over is too much. Overengineering means you develop a feature assuming something will happen in the future. But it rarely happens. Always, and I mean always what you build won't be a rocket ship controller (as long as you don't work building one).

So keep the code as simple as possible, fulfilling it's purpose, being scalable, but not overengineered. Because it's easier to extend a feature rather than rewrite it.

Also don't optimise too early, don't decompose too early, just don't do anything too early. Don't overuse as well. If you just learned about FP don't try to use FP for everything even if it doesn't make sense.

I'd say there's more to it, but these four points are the main points from the rest of the things come from, so if you'll start to master these four points, you'll be already on the senior engineer path.

### Measuring Your Growth Without Guesswork

To measure your progress you can keep a growth journal. It's a kind of journal where you list the things you did to reach your growth goal. For moving into senior mindset you could outline these directions:

* responded to teammate question even though I was in the zone* summarized finding and blockers in a short Slack message before EOD* asked senior developer/principal how he'd solve an issue I found that may have broader effect* explained in PR why I choose simplest working solution rather than overengineering* offer to pair with a junior on their first ticket

Or others you find useful and difficult to do with your current mindset. With such outline you have to become aware when chances appear and use the new approach outlined writing down in the journal what happened, what you did and what were the results.
