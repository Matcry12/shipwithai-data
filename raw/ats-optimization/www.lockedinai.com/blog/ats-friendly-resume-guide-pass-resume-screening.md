## Step 1: Use the Right Keywords
Keywords play a crucial role in how Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) rank resumes. A staggering 99.7% of recruiters rely on keyword filters in their ATS to sort and prioritize candidates. If your resume doesn’t include the right terms, it won’t show up in their searches - no matter how qualified you are. The key is figuring out which keywords matter most and weaving them naturally into your resume.
### How to Identify the Right Keywords
Start by carefully reviewing the job posting. The sections labeled "Requirements", "Responsibilities", and "Qualifications" are treasure troves for keywords. Look for words or phrases that appear multiple times - these are likely the terms recruiters use when searching. Focus on hard skills like technical abilities (Python, Data Analysis, SEO), software proficiency (Salesforce, QuickBooks, AWS), and certifications (PMP, CPA, Six Sigma Black Belt), as 76.4% of recruiters filter candidates based on skills.
Make sure to include the exact job title listed in the posting, like "Senior Project Manager." Resumes featuring the precise job title are 10.6 times more likely to secure an interview. Don’t overlook details like using both acronyms and full forms of qualifications (e.g., "Master of Business Administration (MBA)") since recruiters may search for either.
AI tools can also help streamline the process. Platforms like ChatGPT or Microsoft Copilot can analyze job descriptions and identify the top keywords. Simply paste the job description and ask for the "top 10 relevant ATS keywords". Similarly, resume scanning tools can highlight key terms you might have missed and point out gaps.
> "If the position calls for 'CRM software,' your resume must use those exact words. If you list 'Salesforce,' an ATS will not recognize that as a match."  
>  – James Hu, Founder, [Jobscan](https://www.jobscan.co/)
### Incorporating Keywords Effectively
Once you’ve identified the right keywords, distribute them across your resume - especially in your [resume summary](https://www.lockedinai.com/blog/crafting-strong-resume-summary), experience, and skills sections. This ensures natural repetition without making your resume feel overloaded.
Match the job description’s language exactly. For example, if the posting uses "non-profit" with a hyphen, use the same spelling. If it says "3 years" instead of "three years", stick to that format. Some older ATS systems are sensitive to exact matches, including verb tenses and singular/plural forms. If the job description mentions both "event planning" and "event planner", try to include both to cover all search variations.
Avoid tricks like hidden text or keyword stuffing. Modern ATS software can detect these tactics, and they may raise red flags with human recruiters. Always prioritize readability - after adding keywords, ensure your sentences still flow professionally and make sense.
> "The more often [keywords] appear on your resume, the higher your resume ranks... but that doesn't mean stuffing your resume with keywords in places where they don't make sense."  
>  – Lauren Wethers, Career Coach, [The Muse](https://www.themuse.com/)
To gauge your resume’s ATS compatibility, use scanning tools and aim for a match rate of at least 75%. With these keyword strategies in place, you’ll be better positioned to move on to the next step: formatting your resume so the ATS can read it without issues.
### Prioritize Skills, Job Titles, and Industry Terms
Not all keywords are equal. Skills are the top priority - especially hard skills like technical expertise, specific software tools, and certifications. These are quantifiable and often the first filters recruiters apply. In contrast, soft skills like "team player" are rarely used as search terms.
Job titles are another key factor. Include the target job title in your resume headline or professional summary to show direct alignment. If your previous job title is unconventional, consider adding a more standard industry term alongside it (e.g., "Secretary III/Data Analyst").
Industry-specific terminology also matters. Recruiters often search for exact software names (like "Salesforce" rather than just "CRM"), certifications (such as PMP or CPA), and common abbreviations. Using the right terms demonstrates that you’re familiar with the field and can communicate in the same language as the hiring team.

## Step 2: Format Your Resume Correctly
After identifying the right keywords, the next hurdle is ensuring your resume is formatted so that an ATS (Applicant Tracking System) can actually read it. Even the most qualified candidates can be screened out if their resumes include formatting that distorts or hides critical information. In fact, **98% of qualified candidates are rejected by ATS software because of formatting issues alone**. The good news? These issues are often easy to fix once you know what to avoid.
### Best Practices for ATS-Friendly Formatting
Start with a **single-column layout**. ATS software reads from left to right, and multi-column designs can jumble your information or make sections disappear. Stick to a [chronological or hybrid resume formats](https://www.lockedinai.com/blog/understanding-resume-formats), as these provide the clear timeline ATS algorithms need to analyze your experience accurately.
Choose **standard, web-safe fonts** like Arial, Calibri, Georgia, Helvetica, or Times New Roman. Use 10–12 pt for body text and 14–16 pt for headings. When labeling sections, stick to conventional headings like "Work Experience", "Education", "Skills", and "Certifications." Avoid creative titles such as "My Journey" or "Superpowers", as these might confuse the system.
Place your **contact information at the top of the main document body** , not in headers or footers. Many ATS platforms ignore content in these areas. Save your resume in a **.docx file or text-based PDF**. Avoid using design tools like [Canva](https://www.canva.com/) or Photoshop to export your resume, as these often convert text into images, which ATS software cannot read.
> "ATS systems struggle with creative layouts like two-column designs or graphics. As much as I admire design creativity, ATS systems are ruthlessly utilitarian. If they can't read it, it doesn't exist."  
>  – Stephen Greet, Co-founder, [BeamJobs](https://www.beamjobs.com/)
### Common Formatting Mistakes to Avoid
Certain elements can confuse ATS software or render parts of your resume unreadable. For example:
  * **Tables, text boxes, and sidebars** : These can scramble your content or make sections disappear entirely.
  * **Graphics such as charts, logos, icons, or skill bars** : Most ATS platforms cannot process these, and they may cause your file to appear corrupt.
  * **Custom bullet points** : Stick to standard solid circles or squares. Avoid arrows, checkmarks, or other decorative bullets.
  * Fonts smaller than 10 pt or stylized script fonts: These can cause parsing errors.
  * **Hidden keywords** : Never use tricks like white text on a white background. Modern ATS systems flag this as deceptive and it can damage your credibility.

  
| **Formatting Element**  | **ATS Compatibility**  | **Recommended Alternative**  |  
| --- | --- | --- |  
| Tables & Text Boxes  | Low (Scrambles text)  | Simple line breaks and standard sections  |  
| Multi-column Layouts  | Low (Reads in wrong order)  | Single-column layout  |  
| Images & Logos  | Low (Unreadable/Invisible)  | Text-only descriptions  |  
| Headers & Footers  | Low (Often ignored)  | Place info in the main document body  |  
| Creative Headings  | Low (Misidentifies sections)  | Standard headings (e.g., "Work Experience")  |  
| Special Bullets  | Low (May parse as symbols)  | Standard solid circles or squares  |  
### How to Test Your Resume Format
Before submitting your resume, test its readability. The **Notepad Test** is a simple way to check: save your resume as a "Plain Text (.txt)" file, then open it in Notepad or a similar text editor. If your contact details are missing, bullet points are replaced with odd symbols, or sections are jumbled, it’s a sign that your formatting needs work.
Pay close attention to text that was originally in columns or text boxes - these often appear out of order or merged in the plain text version. If the .txt file looks clean and organized, your resume is likely to pass through most ATS platforms smoothly.
With these adjustments, your resume will be ready to shine in the next step, where you can focus on showcasing your accomplishments.
## Step 3: Showcase Your Accomplishments
Now that your resume has the right format and keywords, it’s time to highlight your achievements. This step not only grabs the attention of recruiters but also makes your resume stand out to ATS systems. This is especially true when using a [functional resume](https://www.lockedinai.com/blog/functional-resume) to emphasize skills over chronological history. Remember, recruiters typically spend just **seven seconds** scanning a resume before deciding whether to keep reading. To make those seconds count, focus on accomplishments rather than routine tasks. For example, instead of saying, "Responsible for managing marketing campaigns", try: "Increased campaign ROI by 200% through A/B testing". This approach demonstrates your impact while incorporating keywords that ATS systems look for.
### How to Write Achievement-Based Bullet Points
Start each bullet point with a **strong action verb**. Using action verbs like "Directed", "Spearheaded", or "Implemented" can boost your chances of landing an interview by **140%**. Avoid vague phrases such as "Responsible for" and replace them with more dynamic language.
Next, include **quantifiable metrics**. Numbers make your accomplishments tangible and help ATS systems prioritize your resume. Use percentages, dollar amounts, timeframes, or team sizes wherever possible. For instance, "Optimized $1.2M budget allocation, improving cost-per-acquisition by 45%" is far more impactful than "Managed budget oversight".
> "Prioritize specific tools, certifications and measurable outcomes – for example, 'SQL, Certified Scrum Master, increased revenue by 30%'."  
>  – Stephen Greet, Co-founder, BeamJobs 
A great way to structure your bullet points is by using the **CAR Method** (Challenge-Action-Result). Break down the problem you faced, the steps you took, and the measurable outcome. For example: "Reduced server response time by 40% by conducting routine performance audits and optimizing MySQL queries".
Also, write in an implied first person. Instead of saying, "I developed marketing strategies", simply write, "Developed marketing strategies that increased lead generation." This keeps your resume professional and concise.  
| Skill Category  | Recommended Action Verbs  |  
| --- | --- |  
| **Leadership**  | Directed, Led, Mentored, Coached, Supervised   |  
| **Problem-Solving**  | Resolved, Improved, Optimized, Streamlined, Troubleshot   |  
| **Communication**  | Presented, Negotiated, Collaborated, Advised   |  
| **Project Management**  | Executed, Implemented, Developed, Organized, Spearheaded   |  
| **Technical Skills**  | Programmed, Engineered, Designed, Built, Automated   |  
Using these verbs, transform your job responsibilities into measurable achievements.
### Turn Job Duties into Results
Ask yourself, "What was the benefit of my action?". Instead of simply listing tasks, explain the outcomes of your efforts. Here’s a comparison of how task-focused bullet points measure up against achievement-focused ones:  
| Task-Focused (Weak)  | Achievement-Focused (Strong)  |  
| --- | --- |  
| Managed marketing campaigns  | Increased campaign ROI by 200% through A/B testing   |  
| Worked with sales team  | Aligned sales-marketing processes, reducing lead conversion time by 30%   |  
| Responsible for budget oversight  | Optimized $1.2M budget allocation, improving cost-per-acquisition by 45%   |  
| Handled customer communications  | Enhanced customer retention by 25% via targeted communication strategies   |  
Focus on your most recent roles, especially those from the last five years. Include **4-5 detailed bullet points** for these positions, emphasizing current tools and methodologies. For older roles, keep it brief or summarize key responsibilities.
Lastly, tailor your language to the job description. If the role mentions "Search Engine Optimization", use that exact term in your resume. For added coverage, you can include both the full term and its abbreviation: "Search Engine Optimization (SEO)". This ensures ATS systems pick up the keyword no matter how they’re programmed.
> "Your resume is typically written in an implied first person without pronouns. This means you avoid using 'I' and instead start bullet points with action verbs (e.g., 'Developed marketing strategies' instead of 'I developed marketing strategies')."  
>  – Clair Levy, Certified Executive Resume Writer 
With your accomplishments clearly outlined, the next step is organizing your skills section to ensure maximum visibility to ATS systems.
## Step 4: Create a Strong Skills Section
Your skills section is one of the most important parts of your resume. Why? Because 76.4% of recruiters rely on ATS (Applicant Tracking System) filters to sort candidates by their skills, and 99.7% use keyword filters to rank applicants. If your skills section isn’t clear and well-organized, you might not make it past the initial screening.
### How to Organize Your Skills for ATS
Start by grouping your skills into clear, recognizable categories like **"Technical Skills"** and **"Soft Skills"**. ATS software is designed to identify standard section titles, so avoid using overly creative or unconventional labels that could confuse the system. Aim to include between 10 and 20 skills that are directly relevant to the role you’re applying for.
Focus on hard skills - these are specific, teachable abilities like software knowledge, certifications, or technical expertise. These are the primary keywords recruiters search for when using ATS filters. For example, instead of something broad like "problem-solving", list specific tools or platforms, such as "SQL", "AutoCAD", or "Adobe Creative Suite".
To ensure your skills are properly recognized by both ATS and recruiters, always include both the full name and its acronym (e.g., _Search Engine Optimization_ (SEO) or _Enterprise Resource Planning_ (ERP)). This way, your resume will match regardless of how the recruiter searches for those terms.
Keep formatting simple and clean. Use bullet points or vertical bars (e.g., Python | Excel | Data Analysis) to separate skills. Avoid using tables, columns, or graphics, as these can confuse ATS systems during parsing. Stick to standard web-safe fonts like Arial or Calibri, and keep your font size between 10-12 points.
Once your skills are organized, refine your list to align with the specific job description.
### Include Keywords in Your Skills Section
Use the exact language found in the job description, including specific job titles and spelling variations. For example, if the job posting mentions "React.js", make sure to write "React.js" exactly as it appears. Similarly, if the listing uses "nonprofit", avoid writing "non-profit." Some ATS systems are sensitive to even small spelling differences and might not recognize synonyms. Including the exact job title from the posting can make your resume 10.6 times more likely to lead to an interview.
Pay close attention to keywords that are repeated throughout the job description - these are likely high-priority skills to include in your resume.
That said, don’t overdo it. Keyword stuffing - repeating the same terms excessively or using "invisible" text - can backfire. Many ATS systems flag this as inauthentic. Instead, integrate keywords naturally into your skills section and throughout your work experience.

| **Soft Skills**  | Collaboration, Troubleshooting, Leadership, Communication  | Include only if explicitly mentioned in the job posting  |  
| **Certifications**  | PMP, CPA, AWS Certified Solutions Architect  | Write both the acronym and the full name  |

## Step 5: Customize and Test Your Resume for Each Job
After fine-tuning the formatting and including relevant skills, the last step is to personalize your resume for each specific job application.
### Why Tailoring Your Resume Matters
Sending out a one-size-fits-all resume won’t get you far, especially with applicant tracking systems (ATS). These systems are designed to scan resumes for exact matches to job descriptions, ranking candidates based on how well their skills, titles, and keywords align with the role. Recruiters also prioritize skills (76.4%), education (59.7%), and job titles (55.3%) when filtering candidates. By customizing your resume to reflect these elements, you can significantly boost your chances of making it through the ATS.
Precision is key. If the job description uses specific terms, make sure your resume mirrors that language. For example, resumes that include the exact job title listed in the posting are 10.6 times more likely to lead to an interview.
### Tools to Test and Optimize Your Resume
Before submitting your application, test your resume to ensure it aligns with the job description. **LockedIn AI's Resume Builder** is a great tool for this - it helps create ATS-friendly resumes with built-in checks for formatting and keyword alignment. The platform ensures your resume uses a clean, single-column layout with standard fonts and section headers, making it easy for ATS systems to process.
Another option is to use resume scanners that provide a "Match Rate" by comparing your resume to the job description. Aim for a score of at least 70%. These tools highlight missing keywords, flag formatting problems like tables or graphics, and offer suggestions for improvement based on the job requirements.
If you prefer a quick manual test, copy your entire resume and paste it into a plain-text editor like Notepad. If the text appears jumbled or out of order, the ATS may have trouble reading it. Also, save your resume as a **.docx file** , as older ATS systems tend to process Word documents more accurately than PDFs.
Once your resume is polished and aligned with the job description, it’s ready for final adjustments based on the specific role.
### Adjusting Your Resume for Each Job
To [make your resume stand out](https://www.lockedinai.com/blog/what-makes-a-resume-stand-out), focus on tailoring it to the unique requirements of each job. Start by pulling 10 to 15 key terms from the job description, especially from the "Requirements" or "Qualifications" sections. These might include hard skills, software tools, certifications, or the exact job title. For example, if the posting mentions "Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP)", include both the full phrase and its acronym.
Update your resume headline or summary to feature the job title exactly as it appears in the posting. Then, integrate the identified keywords naturally into your [work experience and skills sections](https://www.lockedinai.com/blog/showcasing-work-experience-on-resume). Instead of simply listing these terms, demonstrate how you’ve applied them. For instance, write something like, "Boosted sales by 20% using Salesforce CRM" to show measurable results.
Finally, double-check that your section headers are standard and easy to recognize. Titles like "Work Experience", "Education", and "Skills" help ensure the ATS correctly sorts your information.

## FAQs
### How can I find the right keywords to make my resume ATS-friendly?
To craft an ATS-friendly resume, start by thoroughly analyzing the job description for the role you’re targeting. Pay close attention to the _skills_ , _qualifications_ , and _industry-specific terms_ that are mentioned frequently. These are likely the key phrases the ATS is programmed to detect. It’s also a good idea to include both the abbreviated and full versions of terms (e.g., "SEO" and "Search Engine Optimization") to ensure your resume gets recognized.
Integrate these keywords naturally across your resume, particularly in sections like your skills list, work experience, and summary. Be cautious not to overuse them - stuffing your resume with keywords can make it seem artificial and might backfire during a recruiter’s review. Instead, aim for a balanced approach that highlights your qualifications while remaining readable and engaging for hiring managers.
### What formatting mistakes should I avoid to ensure my resume passes ATS screening?
When submitting your resume to an ATS, keep it simple and straightforward. Avoid using intricate layouts, graphics, or unusual fonts, as these can confuse the system and prevent it from accurately reading your details. Stick with clean formatting and standard fonts like **Arial** or **Times New Roman** for the best results.
Make sure to use clear and consistent section headings, such as **Work Experience** , **Education** , and **Skills**. Steer clear of unconventional titles or skipping essential sections, as this can hinder the ATS from pulling out key information. A clean, no-frills layout improves the likelihood that your resume will successfully pass the system and land in front of a hiring manager.
### Why should I customize my resume for every job application?
Customizing your resume for each job you apply to is a must. Why? Because **Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS)** are designed to scan for specific keywords, skills, and formatting that match the job description. Including the exact job title, relevant skills, and industry terms from the posting can significantly boost your chances of making it past the ATS and landing in front of a recruiter.
Keep in mind, ATS algorithms often struggle with resumes that have overly complex layouts, graphics, or inconsistent formatting. These elements can make it harder for the system to read and rank your application. A tailored resume not only helps you clear the automated screening but also demonstrates to employers that you’ve taken the time to understand their needs and align your qualifications accordingly. This level of effort can grab the attention of both the software and the hiring team.
