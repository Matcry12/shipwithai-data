# How to Write an Entry-Level Software Developer Resume

Written by

Updated February 28, 2026

A good resume is an important criterion when applying for a job. It may be challenging to impress prospective employers without a portfolio and with little or no professional experience as a software developer. Understanding how to write an entry-level resume as a software developer may help you identify the skills to highlight when applying for a job. In this article, we explain what an entry-level software developer resume is, discuss how to write a resume for an entry-level software developer, provide helpful tips to guide you, and share a template and an example.

## What is an entry-level software developer resume?

An entry-level software developer resume is a professional document that highlights the qualifications of an entry-level software engineer or developer. This is a professional looking for their first experience in a developer role. Once hired, they work with mid- and senior-level software developers to improve their skills and gain experience. They typically perform routine tasks, such as upgrading existing software, writing simple code for clients, and debugging software. Entry-level developers may advance to higher roles after gaining experience in testing, designing, and managing software.Related: Junior Developer Jobs (With Salaries, Duties, and Skills)Related jobs on Indeed

## How to write an entry-level software developer resume

Here's a step-by-step guide to help you write an entry-level developer resume:### 1. Make a header at the top of your resume

Include the relevant contact information in your header so that potential employers can reach you for questions or an interview. Ideally, there is a link to your portfolio, which may give employers insight into your skills. Typically, you format headers using font sizes 16 to 18. You can set your name in bold to draw attention to it and improve readability. Finally, you can indent the head to the left, right, or centralize it. Typical contents in your header include:- Your full name
- City and province or territory
- Phone number
- Email address
- Link to your website or professional portfolio

### 2. Write a resume objective or summary

Your resume objective provides a summary that highlights your experience. Instead of job experiences, you can include internships or significant projects you completed. You can also describe your strengths and reasons for applying for the role. It's a good idea to streamline your resume summary to the particular job for which you are applying and focus on the key requirements for the position. For example, if the job posting is for a web developer, you can focus on your web design or building skills. An ideal resume summary or objective is typically two or three sentences long.Related:### 3. List your relevant accomplishments and experience

This section allows you to highlight your understanding of software development and technical skills. If you don't have any relevant experience, you can include internships and assistant roles. Add any group project or other creative project experience relevant to the position you want. List each experience with its associated technology, processes, and accomplishments. It's a good idea to match the specific keywords from the job posting when describing your skills.You can use bullet indenting when formatting this section. It is customary to list your experiences or accomplishments in reverse chronological order, meaning from the most recent to the oldest. Then you can use up to six bullet points to highlight your responsibilities under each item. It's advisable to start each point with action verbs, such as "collaborated" or "supervised."### 4. Highlight your education

The relevant information in this section of your resume includes the name of your college or university with the city and province, the name of your major, the type of degree, and the dates you attended. If you have a GPA of 3.2 or higher, you can include it under your degree information alongside any accolades or honours you received.This section is an essential part of your resume because it can show the employer that you qualify and have the required skills and knowledge for the position you want. As a result, it's essential to mention any relevant coursework that shows experience. You can also include training programs, external certification courses, or capstone projects you completed.### 5. List your relevant skills for the role

This section of your resume contains a combination of technical skills, soft skills, and computer skills. When writing this section, it's prudent to focus on your technical and computer skills because these skills may be the most important for this position. Consider including skills you're comfortable talking about so that you can freely discuss them with potential employers during interviews. You may also want to include skills that align with your educational qualifications. Hiring managers typically look for skills that match the job description, show a passion for learning, and show teamwork abilities.Some skills you may include in this section of your resume include:- Web development
- Data analysis
- Time management
- Critical thinking
- Machine learning
- Programming languages
- Teamwork
- Effective communication

### 6. Include additional sections

Although it isn't required, you may want to include different sections to highlight the value you can bring to your potential employer. For example, you might prefer including projects and volunteer activities together in a separate part of your resume. Creating additional sections may help you appear unique from other candidates, inform the employer of your other accomplishments, and show that you're ambitious.### 7. Edit and proofread your work

After you're done writing your resume, it is important to review it and check for grammatical, spelling, punctuation, or formatting errors. Submitting an error-free resume demonstrates your professionalism. You can give the resume to a colleague or family member to proofread or use spelling and grammar checking tools to edit the document.## Tips for creating a resume for an entry-level developer

Here are some tips to help you create your resume as an entry-level software developer:- Structure your resume properly. Using a proper structure may improve the readability of your resume. Consider using one-inch margins, including spaces between the sections, and single-line spacing. Save your resume as a PDF document unless told otherwise.
- Follow formatting instructions. The job posting may have formatting instructions about font size and type. Otherwise, using Times New Roman and font size 11 or 12 is typical for a resume.
- Limit the resume to one page. Hiring managers review many resumes during an application period. It's advisable to limit your resume to one page so the hiring manager can read the contents quickly.
- Consider writing a cover letter. A good cover letter is an excellent support document for your application because it may further explain why you're a good fit for the role. You may consider writing one even if it's not a requirement to give you an advantage over other candidates.
- Quantify your achievements. It's advisable to use percentages and other numerical metrics to quantify the results when highlighting your experience and the application of your skills. Using relevant metrics helps you appear results-oriented and makes your achievements more credible.
