**Here’s how to write an Android developer resume that will even pass the Voight-Kampff test:**
## **1. Choose the Best Format for Your Android Developer Resume**
Recruiters are a bit like robots.
They only spend about 6 seconds scanning your resume to decide if it’s a go or no-go.
You must be adroit enough to rivet their attention right away.
Here’s how you do it:
  * Make the [best heading for your resume](https://zety.com/blog/resume-header)—it’s where you put your [resume contact information](https://zety.com/blog/resume-contact-information).
  * Divide your resume into appropriate [resume sections](https://zety.com/blog/resume-sections).
  * Choose the [best format for your resume](https://zety.com/blog/resume-formats). Your best bet is the [chronological resume layout](https://zety.com/blog/chronological-resume) as it focuses on your most recent achievements and experience.
  * The [fonts for your resume](https://zety.com/blog/best-fonts-for-resume) matter. Focus on readability rather than embellishments.
  * Present the relevant information and use white space to your advantage.
  * Save your [resume as a PDF file](https://zety.com/blog/pdf-word-resume). Don’t go for the MS Word file format unless the job ad asks you for it.


## **2. Write an Android Developer Resume Objective or Summary**
Start your resume with a [profile description](https://zety.com/blog/resume-profile).
What’s that?
A brief paragraph that introduces you to the recruiter. It functions as a short [elevator pitch](https://zety.com/blog/elevator-pitch) and focuses on your professional highlights.
There are two options for you to choose:
**A**[**summary for a resume**](https://zety.com/blog/resume-summary)**is great for a senior Android developer resume** with 2+ years in the workforce. A summary stresses your _experience_ and _achievements_.
**A**[**career objective statement**](https://zety.com/blog/resume-objective)**is suitable for your entry-level Android developer resume.** It emphasizes your _transferable skills_ that prove you’re a great fit.
Regardless of the type of your resume opening, it’s a good idea to put some numbers there to show your real impact.
**Pro Tip** : Even though this section starts your resume, you’re free to write it last. Once your entire Android developer resume is ready, it will be much easier to pick out the highlights.
## **3. Create the Perfect Android Developer Job Description for a Resume**
It’s no secret:
It’s your [resume experience section](https://zety.com/blog/work-experience-resume) that’s the make-it or break-it factor.
It only needs to show one thing—
**You’ll crush it as an Android developer.**
The best way to show it to the hiring manager is by [tailoring your resume to the job offer](https://zety.com/blog/tailor-your-resume):
  * List your latest position up top.
  * Put your [job title](https://zety.com/blog/job-titles), dates worked, company name and locations in each entry.
  * Use bullet points rather than full-length paragraphs. 6-7 bullets per job is enough.
  * Make sure each bullet contains the right [resume words](https://zety.com/blog/resume-action-words), such as _created, reviewed, developed, boosted,_ etc.
  * Focus on your relevant experience and try to put numbers everywhere you can.


**Pro Tip:** If you find it problematic to put a number everywhere, pick out up to 3 bullets you can quantify, and put them in a _key achievements_ subsection.

## **4. Make Your Android Developer Resume Education Section Great**
A great resume is great through and through.
So don’t neglect your [education on a resume](https://zety.com/blog/education-on-resume).
  * **If you have 5+ years of professional experience,** you can only list the school name, degree, and graduation year.
  * **If you have less than 5 years of experience,** you’ll benefit from including info on your academic achievements, extracurricular activities, GPA (if more than 3.5), and relevant coursework.


Your [certifications and licences](https://zety.com/blog/certifications-on-resume) can go straight to a dedicated section so they’re more visible.

**Here’s how to get your Android developer skills right on your resume:**
  * Start with making a master list of [skills for a resume](https://zety.com/blog/what-skills-to-put-on-a-resume). Make sure it includes your [soft and hard skills](https://zety.com/blog/hard-skills-soft-skills), as well as your [technical skills](https://zety.com/blog/technical-skills).
  * Revisit the job offer to see what skills it mentions specifically.
  * Go back to your own list and find the right skills there.
  * Now, choose up to 10 of your top skills, and put them in a _key skills_ section.
  * Sprinkle the entire resume with your skills. Make sure some of them go to your resume header and job description sections.


**Pro Tip:** Focus on your strongest skills. If you add the things you only have a basic understanding of, they’ll devalue your strongest abilities. It’s called the [presenter’s paradox.](https://doi.org/10.1086/664497)
