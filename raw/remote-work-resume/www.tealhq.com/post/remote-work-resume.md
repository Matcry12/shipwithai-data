## What is a work-from-home resume?
The work from home resume **serves the same core purpose as a traditional resume** : It showcases your skills, experience, and qualifications to potential employers. 
However, there are some key differences between the two. For example, remote workers manage time effectively and work independently—a work-from-home resume must demonstrate those relevant soft skills.
As with any job, showcasing experience in the field can help you land more work in the same field. Thus, highlighting experience working from home is helpful to land another remote job.
## How to write a work-from-home resume (key sections to include) 
Writing a resume for remote roles means showing that you can stay focused, work independently, and communicate clearly, even without being in the same room as your team. 
Here’s how to write a work-from-home resume, section by section:
### 1. Contact information
Resumes traditionally include at least the city and state of your address. When applying to remote positions, your physical location is less important. You have a few options:
  * List your city and state
  * Leave it off
  * State “remote” or “location independent”


Always consider the job application at hand. For example, there may be an occasion where you're applying to a work-from-home position but the company requires you to reside nearby. In such a case, list your city and state.
_“I look for an overlap of at least three hours in our business days,”_ says remote work expert and freelance business owner [Domenico Trimboli](https://domenicotrimboli.com/). _“This helps solve issues in a quicker way when they arise.”_
Here's what the contact section of your remote work resume could look like:
_Chris Johnson_
_123-456-7890 |__Chris.Johnson@gmail.com_
_US remote worker in Pacific Time Zone_
### 2. Strong resume objective
Resume objectives, also called a professional summary or resume personal statement, are statements just below your contact information and target title. 
If you are just starting out in the remote field, the resume objective gives you the opportunity to discuss your desire to pivot into a remote [work environment](https://www.tealhq.com/post/ideal-work-environment).
An objective for a work-from-home resume should include your:
  * Desired position or job title
  * Aspirations or career goals (if pivoting into remote work)
  * Experience with remote collaboration and communication


#### Work-from-home resume objective sample
_"Versatile and creative video editor with a passion for visual storytelling, eager to leverage 4 years of experience in crafting captivating video content within remote work environments. Skilled in collaborating effectively with remote teams, utilizing platforms like Slack, Zoom, and Frame.io to facilitate seamless communication and project management._
Example keywords to describe yourself in an objective for a work-from-home resume:
  * Innovative
  * Self-Motivated
  * Adaptabile
  * Tech-Savvy
  * Focused
  * Organized
  * Communicative
  * Reliable
  * Engaged
  * Collaborative


### 3. Remote skills section
A dedicated skills section helps you highlight your qualifications while optimizing your resume for applicant tracking system (ATS) searches using relevant keywords. This approach is especially effective in a [skills-based resume](https://www.tealhq.com/post/skills-based-resume), which puts your abilities front and center. 
Remember to include soft and hard skills that are valuable for remote jobs. Figuring out [how many skills to list on your resume](https://www.tealhq.com/post/how-many-skills-to-list-on-resume) can be tricky, but try to go for quality over quantity. 
#### Soft skills that show you can work without supervision
Your work-from-home resume must convey your ability to manage time without direct supervision. Highlight your self-organization skills, [problem-solving skills](https://www.tealhq.com/post/problem-solving-skills-resume), and capacity to meet deadlines.
Example work-from-home resume soft skills:
  * Time Management
  * Self-Motivated
  * Adaptability
  * Tech-Savvy
  * Problem-Solving
  * Organization
  * Written Communication
  * Initiative
  * Reliability
  * Collaboration


#### Hard skills that prove you’re remote-ready
Your work from home resume must showcase also showcase your [hard skills](https://www.tealhq.com/post/hard-skills-resume) that enable asynchronous communication and collaboration (and tech know-how). 
Example work-from-home resume hard skills:
  * Virtual Collaboration Tools (e.g., Teams, Slack)
  * Video Conferencing Software (e.g., Zoom)
  * Project Management Tools (e.g., Clickup, Asana)
  * Remote Desktop Applications (e.g., TeamViewer)
  * Cloud-Based File Sharing Systems (e.g., Google Drive, Box)
  * Content Management Systems (CMS) (e.g., Wordpress)
  * Customer Relationship Management (CRM) Software (e.g., Salesforce, Hubspot)
  * Online Research Skills
  * Cybersecurity Awareness
  * Password Sharing (1password, LastPass)
  * Knowledge Management Software (Guru, Notion)
  * Screening Recording Software (Loom, BombBomb)


FlexJobs career expert [Toni Frana](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/09/05/dont-forget-this-resume-section-if-youre-looking-for-remote-work.html) advises listing your remote skills “ _after work experience or after the education section, so it’s kind of toward the end of your resume.”_ She also suggests that you label it your “technology or technology skills” section. 
_“This is just a really nice visual for people reading your resume to have a snapshot of your ability to learn new technologies, as well as what you currently know.”_
### 4. Remote work experience
The bulk of your resume is dedicated to listing your professional experience, along with your responsibilities and achievements. But how do you describe remote work experience on a resume? Use the bullet points to highlight your success working in remote positions or online environments. 
Here are some examples:
  * **Highlight productivity:** “Completed customer support tickets, exceeding monthly quota for remote team members”
  * **Highlight awards:** “...as part of a company voted Top Remote Company to Work For 2020”
  * **Highlight self-motivation:** “Liaised with international clients through Salesforce from a dedicated home office.”
  * **Highlight related experience:** Include freelance and self-employed work experience

#### No remote experience? Here’s what to do
Even if you have no remote experience, you can still write a work-from-home resume. Lean on transferable skills you learned from your traditional work environment, such as collaborating with teams in other locations, online tools used, and managing schedules, agendas, or other team members. A [functional resume](https://www.tealhq.com/post/functional-resume) can be especially helpful here, as it focuses on your skills rather than your work history.
## Work-from-home resume tips from hiring experts 
Here’s what hiring managers who build remote teams are really looking for, and how to show it on your resume. 
### Show independent experience, even if it’s unconventional 
David Odier, CEO of Launch Mappers and founder of [Izrom](https://izrom.co), leads fully remote teams and regularly hires people who’ve never worked in a traditional office. What stands out? Side hustles, self-started projects, and freelance gigs.  
  
_“Remote experience comes in different ways,”_ says Odier. _“Someone who leads side projects, or has previous entrepreneurship or freelancing experience are all great indicators.”_
Even if it wasn’t a full-time job, if it shows self-direction, it belongs on your resume. 
### Prove you can communicate without face time 
When everyone’s remote, written communication becomes your most visible skill. Your resume is the first test.  
  
_“Written communication skills are key to making sure remote working works efficiently,”_ Odier explains. _“I'm also looking for confident and ambitious people, which can lead me to think they can manage themselves on a daily basis.”_
### Follow instructions to the letter 
_“Did the candidate 100 percent follow every single part of the instructions?” she says. “If they can’t follow the written directions in an application, they’re probably not right for my virtual team.”_
Her reasoning?_“Ninety percent of [virtual] communication and project briefs are in written form. And the ability to be meticulous is pretty crucial to prevent too much back and forth.”_
‍
**What this means for your resume:**
  * Read the job post carefully and follow every instruction.


  * Use your resume to show how you’ve worked independently and communicated effectively in the past.


  * Don’t underestimate freelance, contract, or side work. Those experiences often show exactly what hiring managers are looking for.

## How to tailor your resume to the remote job description
Customizing your resume to reflect the exact skills, tools, and language in the job post shows you’re not just qualified—you’re a strong fit for _that specific role_.
Here’s how to do it, step by step:
#### 1. Look for repeated themes in the job description
Scan the job post for key phrases, tools, or skills that show up more than once. These are strong indicators of what the company values most.
**Example:**  

If the post says:
  * "Must be proactive and self-directed"
  * "Manage tasks independently with little oversight"
  * "Strong written communication skills"


…then your resume should echo these ideas using similar language.
**Resume bullet examples:**
  * Managed content calendar and met deadlines without supervision
  * Communicated project updates via Slack and email to cross-functional team
  * Took initiative to improve onboarding documentation for remote contractors


#### 2. Match the format of the job requirements
Many job descriptions list qualifications in bullet form. In your Work Experience section, use bullet points to show how you’ve done similar things. Bonus points if you can include metrics that demonstrate the impact of your work!
**Example job description bullet:**
  * Experience with project management tools like Asana or ClickUp


‍**Example tailored resume bullet:**
  * Used Asana to coordinate weekly marketing tasks across remote team; reduced turnaround time by 20%


#### 3. Prioritize relevant experience and reorder accordingly
If the job emphasizes remote client communication or time zone flexibility, move those experiences higher up in your bullet points, even if they’re not the most recent.
Here’s an example: You’re applying for a remote support role that stresses cross-time-zone communication. In your last job, you mostly worked in the office, but two years ago, you worked remotely with global clients.
Instead of listing it last, lead with this bullet:
  * Supported international clients across 4 time zones, coordinating weekly check-ins via Zoom and maintaining a 4.8/5 satisfaction rating


This way, the most relevant experience is immediately visible to the hiring manager.

## ATS formatting for Your remote resume
Building out your resume is half the battle. Getting through Applicant Tracking Systems is the other half. ATS-friendly formatting is boring on purpose. Your goal is simple: make your resume easy for software to parse and easy for a recruiter to skim.
  * **Layout:** Use a clean, single-column format. Skip tables, text boxes, and graphics that can break parsing.
  * **Fonts:** Stick to standard fonts like Arial or Calibri, with consistent sizing and spacing.
  * **Dates:** Use one date style throughout (Month YYYY or MM/YYYY) and keep it consistent across roles.
  * **Headers/footers:** Avoid placing contact info in headers or footers. Some ATS miss it.
  * **Length:** Aim for 1 page if you have under 10 years of experience. Use 1–2 pages if it’s all relevant.
  * **File type:** Submit PDF or DOCX based on the application instructions. For formatting guidance and templates, use [ATS-friendly templates](https://www.tealhq.com/resume-templates/ats).


‍
### Naming, sections, and keyword flow
  * **File name:** Use your name + role, like Jane-Doe-Remote-Customer-Support-Resume.pdf. For more examples.
  * **Section labels:** Use standard headings like Work Experience, Skills, and Education.
  * **Keyword placement:** Put keywords in Skills, echo them in your summary, and prove them in bullets.


‍
### Best practices for keyword placement
  1. Add exact job title and core keywords to the Summary and Skills.
  2. Mirror the posting language in Experience bullets (tools, responsibilities, outcomes).
  3. Make sure 1–2 top keywords show up in the top third of the page .


‍
If you want to sanity-check parsing before you apply, run a scan with a [Resume Scanner](https://www.tealhq.com/tool/resume-checker), because the cleanest format in the world still needs the right signal.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### How do you describe remote work on a resume?
Highlight your experience in effectively managing projects and collaborating with teams in virtual environments. Emphasize proficiency in remote communication tools, meeting deadlines consistently, and delivering results while working remotely.
‍
### What location should you put on your resume for work from home jobs?
For work from home jobs, list your location as "Remote". If time zone is a factor as per job ad, list your city, city and state, or just your time zone. 
### What are the most popular work from home jobs?
Top work from home jobs are related to the tech industry. Top roles are also relative to administrative and data entry, such as virtual assistant, customer service representative, bookkeeper, accountant, and data analyst. You can also find popular remote jobs in content creation and writing, such as social media manager, copywriter, editor, graphic designer, or marketer.
‍
### Do you need a different resume for remote jobs?
Yes, you need a slightly different resume for remote jobs. It should emphasize independence, communication skills, and familiarity with remote tools.
### What should you avoid on a work-from-home resume?
On a work-from-home resume, avoid vague language, irrelevant office-based tasks, or listing tools you haven’t actually used. Stick to clear, job-relevant experience.
