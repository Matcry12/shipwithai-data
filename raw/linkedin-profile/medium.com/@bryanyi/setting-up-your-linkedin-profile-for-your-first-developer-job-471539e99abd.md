For developers searching for their first developer job, I find that **LinkedIn is a way for recruiters/hiring managers to make sure you don't have any red flags.**

As a developer trying to get your first role, I recommend you *optimize every single aspect of your job search.*

This means going far and beyond to thoroughly polish your resume, your projects, your GitHub, your website portfolio, and even your LinkedIn.

**So here is what I thought was the best way to optimize my LinkedIn to get my first developer role.**

There are a ton of ways to do this, but this is what worked for me.

### 1. Using the words "aspiring", "junior", etc…

Writing that you're an "aspiring software engineer" or even a "junior software engineer" is self sabotaging.

Recruiters/hiring managers can already tell on their own if you are in fact a junior, so there's no need to remind them all over your profile.

Just because you might not have a formal job position as a software engineer doesn't mean you're not a software engineer.

So drop those unnecessary adjectives.

### 2. Your Profile Photo

We just need to deal with the fact that **people are judgemental**. If you have a blurry photo that barely shows your face or you're at a weird angle, it'll be unfavorable for you. In other words, it needs to be clean and professional.

The first 4 minutes of [this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BcfGWi8Qywk&ab_channel=JeffSu) will give you really good tips on how to elevate your profile picture.

**I recommend you to basically copy this style where your face is right at the center, it is clearly seen, the picture is bright, and the overall photo is clean.**

Go the extra mile in asking a friend to take a new picture of you if you need to.

### 3. Your Resume, Capstone Project, Website Portfolio, and Github Links

I would recommend you put your resume, your capstone project, your website portfolio, and your GitHub links under the "Featured Section" of your LinkedIn, **in that order**.

Here's an example of what mine looked like:

![None](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:700/1*S6hBsp5ZmdXCmEeIv0c0wA.png)

Bryan's LinkedIn Featured Section Example

Eventually, an engineer is going to have to look through your profile, so figure out which project you consider to be your most impressive project, and **only link that** ***one*** **project** on your LinkedIn's "Featured Section".

Adding your best project as opposed to 3+ projects takes away the guessing game of an engineer checking out your project. If they want to see more of your work, your portfolio website and your GitHub links are right there.

To add a Featured Section:

Underneath your profile picture, click on "Add profile Section" -> Recommended -> Add featured -> click the "+" icon, then add the respective links.

For your resume, choose "Add Media" and use a PDF of your resume so that the thumbnail shows your resume. For the rest, choose "Add a Link" to link your capstone project, your portfolio, and your GitHub.

**Note that the thumbnail of your website portfolio needs to look good too.** It should be cropped and sized to accurately show the thumbnail.

### 4. About Me Section

The most important thing about the "About Me" part is how you convey your passion for software engineering (even if you're not actually passionate about it), and the second is grammar.

Just know that most who come across your page will probably only read the first sentence or two, so it's best to keep it concise.

Here's an example of mine, but I encourage you to look at other LinkedIn profiles to find inspiration on how you can write yours.
> Hey there 👋

> At the end of 2020, my best friend inspired me to become a programmer. He knew that building and creating things was what made me come alive. Since then, I have been using a plethora of online resources to teach myself the basics of software engineering.

> I created a few full-stack projects and a portfolio while maintaining a full-time job as a medical assistant. I am a self-starter with an aptitude for problem-solving, and a passion for applying learned technologies to build pixel-perfect web applications.

> I absolutely love and thrive at the aspect of constantly learning and pondering on challenging problems. Ultimately, I want to create software that positively impacts people's lives. Lately, I've been having a lot of fun with Next.js, Typescript, Tailwind CSS, GraphQL, and NodeJS.

> www.bryanyi.com

### 5. Headline

The headline is that title right below your name. Just put "Web Developer" or "Software Engineer".

I'm aware that many Bootcamp graduates write out their technologies like "*Full Stack Software Engineer | React.js | Javascript | Typescript | Ruby on Rails | Python | Express.js | Node.js*"

**To me, this just screams "I'm a junior…please hire me".**

I understand that you might be conflicted to listen to your BootCamp instructors versus a random guy who wrote a Medium post, but **my issue is that there are** ***so many*** **BootCamp grads doing the** ***exact same thing*** **to market themselves. Ya'll can be spotted from miles away.**

This may seem like a good way to market yourself, but truthfully, companies don't hire recruiters to find junior developers.

If you absolutely want to share the technologies you know, you can add it in your "About Me" section from #4.

**Companies will know you're interested when you apply for their job and network with engineers.**

I'd just want to know what technologies you used, and what you were able to build with it. If this impresses me, I'm not worried about you having to learn my company's tech stack.

### 6. Education

Remove the information about when you graduated since that can give away your age. We want to remove any room for bias from a recruiter or hiring manager.

If you didn't go to college, just leave this empty. Definitely do not put your high school here!

LinkedIn is a small part of the overall job search. It's obviously more important that you have very good projects, a solid website portfolio, and a professional resume, **but that doesn't mean you shouldn't create a professional LinkedIn profile where people can learn more about you.**

The tips above are a combination of what I did and what I know *now* being on the other side of reviewing resumes and reviewing other people's LinkedIn profiles.
