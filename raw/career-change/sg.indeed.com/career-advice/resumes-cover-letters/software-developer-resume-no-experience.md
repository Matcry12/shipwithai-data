If you're a software developer who's new to the workforce or looking for your first job, then writing a resume is necessary for job applications. Yet, there are plenty of ways that you can showcase your skills and abilities even if you don't have a lot of professional experience. Knowing what to include in your resume and how to structure it can help you impress employers regardless of your qualifications.In this article, we explain how to write a software developer resume with no experience, provide a resume template and an example and share five tips you can follow when creating your own resume.Related: [How to Make a Career Change to be a Software Developer](https://sg.indeed.com/career-advice/finding-a-job/career-change-to-software-developer)
## How to write a software developer resume with no experience
This is a seven-step guide on how to write a software developer resume with no experience:
### 1. Start with a header
Begin your resume by creating a header that has your name and contact information. You can place this at the very top of your document. After you mention your first and last name, you can include relevant information such as your email address, phone number and a link to your online portfolio or website if you have one. Place your header at the top of the resume on the left, centre or right of the page.Related: [What Is a Resume? (Plus How to Write and Format a Resume)](https://sg.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/what-is-a-resume)
### 2. Write a summary statement
The next section is your summary statement. This is a brief paragraph that summarises your skills and experience as a software developer. You can use this section to explain why you're interested in this work and what you hope to gain from the experience. Include your education level and any relevant certifications or training that you have. This section is important because it's essentially an introduction to who you are as a professional. Therefore, you want the employer to have a good first impression of you.
### 3. List your education and certifications
Next, you can include your education history and any relevant certifications or awards. If you have a degree in computer science or a similar technical field, make sure to mention it. Also include any certifications you earned that are specific to software development, such as programming languages or software development platforms.You may also want to include a coursework section that lists the technologies and programming languages you studied in school. As this is a resume for applicants with no work experience, you can list the education section first before you outline your employment history.Related: [How to Build Education Section in Your Resume](https://sg.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/education-on-resume)
### 4. Add relevant experience
Many software developers start their careers with little or no experience, but if you do have some, be sure to include a section that highlights it. If you don't have any software development experience, consider adding a section dedicated to your qualifications with technology.This can include courses you took, personal projects that you worked on or skills you obtained through internships or work experience in a different field. Alternatively, simply include any experience you have that might be relevant to the job, such as freelance work or programming projects.Related: [How To Obtain Your Employment History and Why It Matters](https://sg.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/employment-history)
### 5. Highlight your skills
To be a successful software developer, it's important to have strong technical skills, including programming languages, software tools and analytical thinking. Non-technical skills, such as strong communication, are also beneficial qualities to have.On your resume, make sure to highlight the skills that can help differentiate yourself from other candidates. If you're applying for a specific job, make sure to include the skills required for that position. You can find these in the job description by looking at what keywords the employer uses.Related:


### 6. Add any additional information
If you have any additional information that you think might be useful for employers to know, you can include it at the bottom of your resume. This might concern any languages you know or your relevant hobbies and interests. This gives the employer a chance to get to know you better and can help showcase your passion.Related: [How to Write a Full-Stack Developer Resume (With Example)](https://sg.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/full-stack-developer-resume)
### 7. Proofread your work
Before you submit your resume, make sure to proofread it for any errors. Check your grammar, punctuation and spelling. You can also check to ensure that the formatting is consistent throughout the document.If you're not confident in your editing skills or you just want to make sure that your work is perfect, you can always have someone else read over your resume to spot any mistakes you missed. Consider asking a friend or family member for help.Related: [How To Write a Python Developer Cover Letter (With Example)](https://sg.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/python-developer-cover-letter)

## Tips for writing a resume
Here are some tips to remember when you're writing your own resume:
### Pay extra attention to the education section
When writing your resume, place particular focus on your education section. If you have no prior experience, then highlighting your education and relevant coursework is key to demonstrating your potential as a software developer. That's why it's advisable that you place this section towards the top of the resume, namely before you outline your experience and skills.
### Use strong action verbs
When describing your experience and skills, remember to use strong action verbs. These are words that can help demonstrate your abilities in a more concrete and positive way. For example, instead of simply saying I have experience with Java, try using words like demonstrated or utilised which can improve the impact of your resume. Other good action verbs include:
  * provided
  * managed
  * designed
  * built
  * coded
  * analysed
  * tested
  * communicated
  * collaborated
  * developed

Related: [139 Action Verbs to Make Your Resume Stand Out](https://sg.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/action-verbs-to-make-your-resume-stand-out)
### Highlight your strengths and interest in the field
When writing your resume, focus on highlighting your strengths and any relevant interests you have in the field of software development. This can help show potential employers that you have the ability to be successful in the role. Also, if you have any personal projects related to software development, you can include these as well.
### Keep it simple and straightforward
When writing your resume, try to keep the language simple and straightforward. This helps make sure that your resume is easy to read and understand for potential employers.
