## How to Write a Career Change Resume

A career change resume needs to answer one question before anything else: why should an employer consider you when there are candidates with direct experience? The answer lies in your transferable skills, your demonstrated commitment to the new field, and the story you tell in your professional summary. Career changers who earn interviews lead with their new direction confidently, show concrete preparation steps, and draw explicit connections between what they've already mastered and what the new role requires.

### 1. Write a Targeted Professional Summary

Your summary is where you control the career change narrative. State your new career direction, the most relevant transferable skills you bring, and the concrete steps you've taken to prepare — courses, certifications, or projects. Don't apologize for the change. Frame it as a deliberate, informed decision that makes you a stronger candidate.

### 2. Use a Skills-Forward Format

A traditional chronological resume may work against you if your job titles are all in a different field. Consider a hybrid format: open with a strong skills section and summary, then list experience chronologically. This puts your relevant capabilities front and center before a recruiter reaches your job history.

### 3. Demonstrate Field-Specific Preparation

The biggest concern about career changers is whether they're serious. Eliminate that doubt by showing concrete preparation: certifications earned, courses completed, portfolio projects built, networking events attended, or volunteer work done in the new field. Even a 3-month online course shows more commitment than a stated interest.
