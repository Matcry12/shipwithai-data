## What are transferable skills?
[Transferable skills](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/career-development/top-job-skills) are core abilities you develop through various experiences such as previous jobs, volunteer work, education or everyday life. They remain relevant no matter the role or industry. These skills support your ability to adapt, solve problems and contribute effectively in new environments. Because these skills are not tied to a specific job function, they’re especially useful when exploring new career paths or industries.For example, problem solving, time management and adaptability are all transferable skills that can help you succeed in a wide range of professions. A retail employee who resolves customer concerns efficiently may apply those same skills in a client-facing role in tech or healthcare. Similarly, someone who manages multiple deadlines in an academic setting can bring valuable time management skills to a fast-paced office environment.

## 10 examples of transferable skills
Before making a career change or applying for a new role, it’s important to identify the transferable skills you already have. If you’re not sure where to start, review job descriptions for your target position and note the key skills employers are looking for. Below is a list of transferable skills you can use as a starting point for assessing your own skillset:
### 1. Communication
Strong communication is the ability to impart information to others through speech, writing or another medium. [Communication skills](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/communication-skills) help you know when and how to ask questions, how to read body language and how to talk to people in many contexts. In the workplace, employers value strong communicators for their ability to work with others and move projects forward.Transferable communication skills include:
  * Confidence
  * Nonverbal communication
  * Responsiveness
  * Public speaking


### 2. Dependability
Dependability encompasses qualities that make you a trusted employee. These include punctuality, organization and responsibility. Every employer seeks candidates who can be trusted to accomplish tasks well and in a timely manner. They often trust these same people to manage relationships, assignments and goals successfully.Transferable dependability skills include:
  * Punctuality
  * Self-motivation
  * Honesty


### 3. Teamwork
[Teamwork](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/team-player-on-resume) skills involve the ability to work with others towards a common goal. Effective teamwork requires several other qualities, such as empathy, active listening and strong communication. Providing examples of successful teamwork during interviews can help employers understand how you’ll work with others in their company.Transferable teamwork skills include:
  * Effective communication


### 4. Organization
A well-organized person has a strong, neat structure in their workspace, tasks and relationships. [Organized employees](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/career-development/organization-skills) typically meet deadlines, communicate with others in a timely manner and follow instructions well. Employers can trust organized workers to meet deadlines, take notes and ensure efficient project completion.Transferable organization skills include:
  * Project management skills
  * Budgeting


### 5. Adaptability
[Adaptability skills](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/career-development/adaptability-skills) enable employees to stay focused and productive even as teams, projects, management or products change. Employers value flexible candidates who can quickly learn new skills and processes, especially when facing new challenges, to ensure work is completed efficiently and with a positive attitude.Transferable adaptability skills include:
  * Problem-solving skills
  * Patience


### 6. Leadership
[Leadership skills](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/leadership-skills) include traits like strong communication, relationship building and dependability. You can transfer leadership skills to many different industries because most employers value people who can organize teams to reach shared goals.Transferable leadership skills include:
  * Team building
  * Risk-taking
  * Goal setting


### 7. Decision-making
[Decision-making](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/career-development/decision-making-skills) is appealing to employers across the board as it shows confidence and good judgment, which are valuable in any industry. Decision-making requires you to analyze a situation, predict possible outcomes and come up with a solution or action in an efficient time frame.Transferable decision-making skills include:
  * Analytical skills


### 8. Empathy
Having strong empathy and emotional intelligence promotes a healthy work environment and helps build strong relationships with clients and colleagues. As many jobs involve working or interacting with other people, having [empathy](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/career-development/how-to-be-empathetic) is a skill that is desirable to most employers.Transferable empathy skills include:
  * Perspective taking
  * Curiosity


### 9. Initiative
Being able to motivate yourself in the workplace is a skill that’s impressive regardless of the industry you’re in. Taking the [initiative](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/career-development/ways-to-take-initiative-at-work) to pursue new tasks, contribute ideas and produce high-quality work helps show commitment to your job and often results in career advancement.Transferable initiative skills include:
  * High-achiever
  * Process improvement
  * Eagerness


### 10. Technical skills
Technical skills and technology literacy are your competencies when it comes to navigating new technology. Nearly every position in every industry will require the use of technology at some level. In an increasingly technological workplace, employers value candidates who can learn new tools and software quickly to complete tasks.Transferable technology literacy skills include:
  * Troubleshooting
  * Point-of-sale software
  * Customer relationship management software
  * Productivity software

## How to highlight transferable skills during a job search
You can showcase transferable skills throughout your job application, including your resume, cover letter and interviews. Start by carefully reviewing the job description and job title to identify which of your transferable skills best match the position. Tailoring your application this way helps recruiters quickly see how your experience aligns with the role.
### On your resume
There are several effective ways to include transferable skills on your [resume](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/what-to-include-on-a-resume). Depending on the job title you're targeting, you can highlight these skills in the following sections:
#### Resume summary or objective
Include your most valuable and relevant transferable skills right at the top of your resume in your summary or objective statement.Example:  
Tenacious project manager with five years of experience using strong communication skills to complete successful end-to-end projects across several teams.
#### Work experience
Focus on the skills you used to achieve key accomplishments in previous roles. Instead of listing tasks, highlight two or three results that demonstrate your abilities. You don’t need to name the skill directly, let the accomplishment speak for itself.Example:  
Established competitive quotas and a bonus program for the sales department, increasing YoY revenue by 10% in the most recent fiscal year.
#### Skills list
Use this section to display your strongest transferable skills. Refer to the job description for guidance—look at sections like “job duties” or “requirements” to see what employers are looking for, and align your list accordingly.
### In your cover letter
When writing your cover letter, focus on one or two transferable skills that are mentioned in the job description. Use the body of your letter to highlight how you’ve applied these skills in previous roles.Example:  
In my previous role at Crane & Jenkins, I served as head bookkeeper for over five years, managing all financial records. During that time, the company saw an 18% increase in revenue. I also collaborated closely with other administrators and thrived in a team-oriented environment.In this example, the candidate demonstrates organization and teamwork—two valuable transferable skills. By sharing specific results, they help the employer clearly see how those skills can benefit the new role.
### During interviews
In your interview, use specific examples to demonstrate how you’ve applied relevant transferable skills. Focus on showing, not just telling. Share clear, concise stories that highlight your strengths in action.For example, if you’re asked about working with others, you might describe a time when your [interpersonal skills](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/interpersonal-skills) helped resolve a conflict or improve team collaboration. As you explore new opportunities, you’ll likely find that many of your current skills can easily transfer to new roles and industries.Related: [How to Write a Skills-Based Resume: Template and Examples](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/skills-based-resume-template)
## How to improve transferable skills
Improving your transferable skills, also known as portable skills, can help you stand out to employers, especially when changing roles or industries. Here are several effective ways to strengthen these skills:
### Take on new responsibilities
Volunteering for tasks outside your usual role, such as managing a project or leading a meeting, can help you build [soft skills](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/soft-skills) like communication, leadership and time management.
### Pursue professional development
Online courses and training programs are great for developing specific skills. For example, taking a course in Excel can boost your data organization and problem-solving abilities, which are valuable in almost any role. You can also explore free or paid learning options shared on platforms like LinkedIn Learning.
### Gain experience through internships
Internships offer hands-on opportunities to build and apply transferable skills in real-world settings. Even short-term or part-time roles can help you grow in areas like teamwork, adaptability and attention to detail.
### Seek feedback
Ask managers, mentors or coworkers for input on your strengths and areas for growth. Constructive feedback can help you identify which transferable skills to focus on and how to improve them.
### Practice regularly
Like any skill, transferable skills improve with consistent use.
