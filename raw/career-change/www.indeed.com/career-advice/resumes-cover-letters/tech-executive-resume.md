## What is a tech executive?
Technology executives oversee computer information systems within an organization. They may be a part of strategizing program updates or troubleshooting computer systems. A tech executive may have the following duties:
  * Manage a team of other information technology professionals
  * Oversee all technology departments
  * Provide recommendations on how to improve computer systems within an organization
  * Manage the IT budget
  * Keep up with industry regulations and trends in technology
  * Communicate with technology vendors to meet the needs of the company
  * Train new technology employees
  * Monitor security of computer systems
  * Troubleshoot technology systems within the business
  * Present technology findings and systems in executive team meetings

## What to include in a tech executive resume
It's important to include certain types of information in your tech executive resume to show why you're a good fit for the position. This is your opportunity to highlight the specific academic accomplishments, work experiences and skills in both information technology and leadership that would make you the right executive leader for the technology department.A tech executive resume typically includes the following:
  * Professional summary: A professional summary goes at the top of your resume. Use this section to specifically highlight any technical or leadership experience that is most relevant to the position.
  * Core skills: List your relevant skills directly after your professional summary. This ensures the hiring manager can easily review the skills you have that are relevant.
  * Work experience: Work experience follows skills. Start with the most relevant position, including your specific job duties that will help in the current position.
  * Volunteer experience: You may include volunteer experience in place of work experience if you have limited experience. List it in the same position as professional experience, following skills, but be sure to include that it's volunteer experience.
  * Education: Education typically follows work experience. Use this section to list your degrees or certifications, specifically in information technology or business management.
  * Professional memberships: Professional membership goes after the academic experience. You can use this section to highlight your dedication to the IT industry.
  * Professional certifications: Professional certifications can go on the same list as professional memberships. Use this section to demonstrate any certifications you have.
  * In-progress training: You may also include any certifications or training that you're currently working toward. Include this with certifications.

## How to write a tech executive resume
You can write a tech executive resume by following these steps:
### 1. Collect all of your information
Before creating your resume, it's beneficial to collect all of your information in one place. This includes names, locations, dates of employment and primary duties of each work position. This may include both paid or unpaid experiences. Consider also including the titles or dates of any professional certifications or memberships you are a part of in technology or as an executive.Related: [Listing Professional Experience on Your Resume](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/listing-professional-experience-on-your-resume)
### 2. Consider your career goals
Consider your career goals to prepare for writing your professional summary. The professional summary goes at the top of your resume and is often the first thing that the hiring manager will read. Try to connect your summary to the specific industry or position to which you're applying. For example, if you want to work in retail, you might discuss technology achievements or goals as they relate to this industry.Related: [A Complete Resume Summary Guide (40+ Examples)](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/writing-a-resume-summary-with-examples)
### 3. Input your details
Once you collect your information, you can format it into a resume. You might use a template or create a resume with a logical flow for the position to which you're applying. Be sure to include all relevant technology expertise, managements details and work experiences.
### 4. Give educational background
If you have any certificates or degrees, include this after your work history. You can also include any education or training currently in progress by listing the date you intend to graduate. You might also include student club memberships or honors society awards in the section of your resume. Some candidates may also list specific classes that are relevant to the position.If you have more than 10 years of work experience, you don't necessarily have to include a graduation date on your resume.
### 5. Include achievements or certifications
If you have any professional achievements or certifications in addition to your experience, you might include them on your resume in a separate section. Be sure to include the name of the certification and the organization that provided the certification. You can also include the date of completion and how long your license or membership lasts.
### 6. Highlight your technical and leadership skills
A tech executive uses both technical and leadership skills to manage the information technology systems of a business. This means it's important to highlight both types of skills equally. Include both soft and hard skills when listing your most relevant skills. For example, you might include things like leadership or project management, which are especially useful as an executive. You might also include specific technological skills like troubleshooting or software development.

## Tech executive resume tips
Here are a few tips you can use when creating a tech executive resume:
  * Be specific when listing skills. Try to be as specific as possible when listing skills. This gives hiring managers a better understanding of the programs or computer systems that you can use.
  * Use descriptive language for all levels. It's also important to be descriptive but inclusive. Hiring managers or interviewers may not have the same level of technical knowledge as you, so consider writing your resume in a way that all levels of team members can understand.
  * Include accomplishments. Executive team members are often responsible for staying within budgets and meeting goals. When possible, provide more information about your experiences by including specific accomplishments with a dollar amount or percentage, along with primary duties.
  * Consider a key achievement section. Depending on where you are at in your career, you may have a long list of achievements. You can include them in a separate section at the end of your resume.
